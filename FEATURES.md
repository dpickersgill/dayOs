# Verdynt/OS features

What each part of the app does and the rules it enforces, grouped by area. For running the app see [README.md](README.md); for deploying see [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md); for security and operations see [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md).

## How the platform works

### How tenancy and access work

- Every business table has `tenant_id`, and every foreign key includes it, so a row cannot reference another mountain's data even by mistake.
- `tenant_id` is read only from the signed JWT, never from the URL or body. Every query filters on it.
- Roles: `owner`, `manager`, `counter`, `instructor`, `patrol`, `staff`. Owner can do everything; each route lists who else may use it. Only the owner can create managers.
- The one unauthenticated read is `GET /api/v1/public/:slug/conditions` (operating status, lifts, trails, upcoming events). It exposes no people, prices, or notes beyond the public day note.
- Pricing is always computed on the server. Clients never send prices.
- Sessions are checked against the database on every request, so deactivating a login, changing its role, or changing its password takes effect immediately, not when the token expires. Changing or resetting a password signs out every other session.
- **Signing in**: mountain, then **username or email**, then password. Every login has a username (unique at the mountain, 3 to 40 letters, numbers, dots, dashes or underscores, case does not matter); a login with an email can use either. Email is optional except for owners and managers, who must keep one. New logins get a username made from the linked staff member's name (first initial and last name, "mellis"), which the manager can change; anyone can change their own on Account. Existing logins were given one the same way when this arrived, and their email still works.
- **Temporary passwords**: a manager (or the platform console, for owners and managers) sets a password with "Temporary" ticked, tells the person in person or by phone, and on their next sign-in they must choose their own before anything else opens (the server refuses every other request until then). New logins are made the same way by default. This is how someone with no email gets back in; there is no reset by email.
- A login with no email is shown by its username wherever a name is shown (who did what, Inbox, case notes).
- A `staff` login is linked to one staff record (`users.staff_id`). Non-managers can only clock in, set availability and view certifications for that record, and a login without a link cannot clock in.
- Sensitive reads (patrol incidents) and all writes are recorded in `audit_log`.

### Keeping mountains apart

Every mountain's data carries its `tenant_id`, and the signed-in mountain comes only from the login token. `test/tenant-isolation.test.js` proves it: it fills one mountain with demo data, signs in as a second mountain, and calls every signed-in endpoint with the first mountain's ids. It fails if anything of the first mountain comes back, changes, or gets linked to the second. It also checks that a leak would be caught. Run it after adding any new endpoint (`npm test` includes it). It reads the route files itself, so new endpoints are covered automatically; if you add a new route file, add it to the `MOUNTS` list at the top of the test.

### The platform console

Open `https://<your-service-address>/platform` and enter the platform key (the `BOOTSTRAP_KEY` setting; it is kept only in that browser tab). From there you can:

- **Add a mountain**: name, web name, owner login, document storage limit, and which apps it starts with.
- **Turn apps on and off per mountain**: Sell, Products and prices, Gate, Lifts and trails, Terrain park, Rentals, Ski school, Clubs and programs, Instructor availability, Patrol, Documents, Money, and Staff and schedule, plus the paid add-on **Document text recognition**, which starts off for every mountain. An app that is off disappears from the menu for everyone at that mountain (owner included), its requests are refused, and its public pages answer "not found". Nothing is deleted, so turning it back on restores everything. Sell needs Products and prices, so the console turns them on and off together. The home page, customers, account pages and the daily summary are always on. Changes show within about 10 seconds.
- **Pause a mountain**: everyone is signed out, nobody can sign in, and its public pages stop. Resume at any time.
- **Set the document storage limit** (default 500 MB). Uploads past the limit are refused with a clear message; the mountain sees its usage on the Documents screen.
- **Reset an owner or manager password** (and turn off their two-step sign-in if the phone was lost). That person is signed out everywhere.
- **See recent activity**: every change made in the console is logged.

A mountain that has never been touched in the console has every app on. The console is not tied to any mountain's logins: a mountain owner's token cannot open it. Use a long random `BOOTSTRAP_KEY`.

### Try-it demo

Visitors can start their own demo at `/try`: they enter a name and email, and about 20 seconds later they are signed in to a private demo mountain with every app on and made-up lifts, tickets, lessons, patrol cases, staff and documents. It is a real mountain (named `try-xxxxxxxxxx`) built by the same loader as `node db/demo.js`. The loader can run after `node db/seed.js` and again on the same mountain without adding anything twice. Its ski school has four instructors with certifications and pay rates, kids and adult lessons on each of the next 7 days, and a six-week Saturday program; staff get 6-digit kiosk PINs and patrollers a published week on the patrol schedule.

- **Expires:** after `SANDBOX_DAYS` days (default 7). A finished demo cannot sign in. It is removed with every row it owns the next time anyone starts a demo, or when you run the purge (below). The sign-up row stays, so `/platform` > **Demo sign-ups** is your list of leads.
- **Safe:** a demo can never send email or take a card payment, has a 25 MB document limit, and is hidden from the main mountain list in `/platform`. Starting again with the same email replaces that person's old demo.
- **Limits:** 5 demos per address per hour (`SANDBOX_RATE_LIMIT`), at most 40 running at once (`SANDBOX_MAX_ACTIVE`), and at most 3 being built at the same moment. A hidden form field turns away simple bots.
- **Link back to your website:** set `MARKETING_URL` (for example `https://summitops.example/contact`) and the tag inside a demo ("Demo mountain. 6 days left.") shows an "Ask for a walkthrough" link to it.
- **Daily cleanup (optional):** `POST /api/v1/platform/sandboxes/purge` with the `X-Platform-Key` header removes finished demos. Run it from Cloud Scheduler once a day.
- This release adds a column and a table, so run `npm run migrate` when upgrading. `test/sandbox.test.js` covers sign-up, expiry, full removal, the limits and the email block.

- **One row height for everyone** on a computer (34px rows, the same for a counter as for an owner).
- **Pinned at the bottom:** Settings, Help, your name with the light or dark switch, Sign out and the version.
- **Only the middle scrolls**, and its edge always rests between two rows: its height is cut to the last whole row that fits, never on a heading. A thin line and soft shadow mark an edge with more past it (no fade over the rows). The page you are on is scrolled into view, with a row of room past it, under the pinned top. The whole sheet scrolls as one.

Once something is typed it also finds the **tabs inside apps** (row 1 and row 2: Opening checks, Lift inspections, Lift evacuation, Today's posts, Daily map, Records requests, Access log and so on), each shown with its app ("Lift inspections in Mountain status"), and only those the person may open: Patrol's pages follow the person's Patrol access (`/patrol/me`, loaded when Jump to opens). ### In-app help
**Help** is in the side menu under your name, and a **Help with this page** link sits under every page title. It holds short how-to articles for each app: numbered steps, a few tips and related articles, with a search box that looks through titles and text.
- People see only the topics their role can use, for apps their mountain has turned on. A mountain without Rentals has no Rentals help.
- The articles are plain data in `public/js/help-content.js` (a section per app, then articles with `steps`, `tips` and `related`). To add or change one, edit that file; `test/help.test.js` checks that ids are unique, links point at real articles and every section is a real screen.
- No server or database changes.

### Look, words and times in the app (fix/app-policy)
- **Brand colors.** Light theme: primary buttons, badges and counts are the brand gold (#d8b672) with dark slate text (8:1); links are spruce and underlined; lines, stripes and switch tracks use a darker gold that holds 3:1 on white. The dark theme uses the same gold. Every color in `public/css/app.css` is a custom property on `:root` (dark values under `:root[data-theme='dark']`), and corners use one scale: `--radius-xs` 4, `--radius-sm` 6, `--radius` 8, `--radius-md` 12, `--radius-lg` 14, `--radius-xl` 16 px and `--radius-pill`. `--trail-blue` stays as an alias for older code.

- **One time format.** Times read 12-hour with AM or PM on the mountain's clock ("10:58 AM"). The server's shared formatter is `clock()` in `src/lib/util.js` (Today's morning board, report checks, care notes); the screens use `fmtTime` and, for UTC timestamps such as sign-ins and devices in Settings, `fmtInstant` in `public/js/ui.js`.

- **Printing a case.** Care, Report and Record print one after the other, Care under the case header on page 1 and the others each on a new page. Buttons, helper text, "(13 min ago)", open dialogs and the sticky bars are left off; date and time boxes print as words ("Sat, Nov 7, 6:54 PM ET") through `printableTimes()`; report sections print open.
- **Phones.** At 600 px and narrower, buttons and check box or radio labels are at least 44 px tall, and map zoom buttons are 44 px everywhere. The passkey prompt waits until no other dialog is open.

### Versions and What's new
Every release has a calendar version (YEAR.MONTH.N, for example 2026.10.4) and a build (the git commit). The version shows on the sign-in screen, in the account menu and at the foot of Help; `/version` and `/readyz` report it.
- **Help > What's new** lists each release, newest first, with only the notes for the person's role and the apps their mountain has on. Releases that hold nothing for them are left out.
- A small dot on Help (and on the account button, and the phone's More tab) means there is something new since they last looked. Opening What's new clears it. Someone who has never opened it sees the dot for the newest release only. Stored per login in `users.seen_version`.
- The notes live in `src/release/changelog.json`; `test/release.test.js` checks it is well formed, newest first, and matches `package.json`.
- The console's **Releases** shows the live version, the deploy history (`app_deploys`: each version and build, first and last start, who deployed it) and every note, including console-only ones.
- `deploy.sh` refuses to deploy an older version over a newer one unless given `--allow-older`, and checks afterwards that the new version is the one answering.

### Business rules enforced by the code

- **Price precedence:** date+tier override, then date override for all tiers, then tier price, then base price. A blackout blocks the sale.
- **Scans:** log every scan with a result (`ok`, `expired`, `not_yet_valid`, `void`, `no_uses`, `unknown`). Punch cards decrement.
- **Refunds:** void unused tickets; refused if any ticket on the order was already scanned.
- **Rentals:** items are locked and checked in one transaction (no double checkout); a signed waiver for the current season is required; damaged items go to maintenance.
- **Shifts and lessons:** staff must be active, hold every required certification (unexpired on the day), have no overlapping shift or lesson, and fit declared availability (managers can override availability only, never certifications).
- **Capacity:** lesson classes and programs are enforced under row locks. Full programs waitlist, and a withdrawal promotes the next person.

### Safe against double taps

The database itself refuses a second open till drawer or a second open clock-in for the same person, drawer moves are locked against closing, a cancelled lesson booking cannot be revived into a full lesson, and a transaction that loses a lock collision is retried automatically. `test/races.test.js` fires bursts of simultaneous requests to prove it.

## Customers

### The shared customer record

`people` is the one customer table every module points at: tickets and orders (`orders.person_id` is the purchaser), lesson bookings, rentals, programs, waivers, and patrol patients (patrol sees name, phone, email and date of birth only). It holds name, email, phone, date of birth, postal address, emergency contact, ski or snowboard and ability, and email consent (with the time it was given).

- **Capture:** the sell screen and the lesson roster show the same customer form. Choosing an existing customer fills it in so the clerk can confirm or correct it.
- **Matching:** with no customer chosen, a purchaser is matched by email, or by name plus date of birth. A match is reused and only its blanks are filled; nothing already on file is overwritten and consent is never withdrawn by a match. No match creates a customer. Without an email or date of birth, the same person can be recorded twice.
- **Mandatory details:** a manager chooses, separately for ticket sales and lesson bookings, which details are required, and can require a purchaser on every sale (`customer_form_config`). Names are always required once someone is recorded. With nothing chosen, only names are required.
- **Consent:** the email box is never ticked by default and cannot be made mandatory. `GET /people/export.csv?marketing=1` lists only customers who agreed.
- **Consent history (release 1):** every change is a row in `consent_events` (email or text, agreed or opted out, how, who, when); `people.marketing_opt_in` follows the latest email row. Existing consent was carried over once as source "earlier record". `GET/POST /people/:id/consent`.
- **Alerts and notes:** `person_flags` (do not rent, unpaid balance, medical alert, VIP, banned, note; optional end date; managers set and clear) show in the customer record and in the customer picker. Patrol sees medical alerts only. `person_notes` are dated, with author; the old single note was carried over as the first entry. They warn staff; they do not block a sale or rental.
- **Privacy:** `GET /people/:id/data-export` (manager, audited) downloads one person's data; `POST /people/:id/anonymise` (manager, audited) removes identity and keeps sales, tickets, rentals, signed waivers and the consent history. Merge and anonymise leave a `person_tombstones` row. Blocked for staff, sign-in holders and gear still out.
- **Privacy, ski school (2026.11.13):** the download and anonymise also cover the rows that hang off a child's lesson bookings: the adaptive intake and the pickup list are deleted; a lost child report stays as the school's safety record without the jacket, helmet and last-seen words; a family's 0 to 10 answer stays in the score without its comment; who the child was released to, the release reason and the booking-past-a-warning note are cleared. Group program rosters (the parent's contact), the group leader and chaperones are matched on email or phone. Class-wide live updates and group places are not about one guest (live updates are deleted after 90 days, places after the day). Scorecard results from the website are Verdynt/OS leads, not a mountain's guests.
- **Data quality:** `GET /people/quality` (manager) counts what is missing; `GET /people/scores` gives each listed customer a completeness percent. Code: `src/routes/customerplus.js`, `src/lib/consent.js`, `db/customers.sql`. Not built yet: retention setting, tags and saved groups, households, organisations (see the Customer Record Gap Review).

### Finding a customer, and the patrol pull

- Every screen that asks "who is this?" uses one search box: type a name, a phone number or an email. `GET /people/lookup?q=` returns the best
  matches ranked (an exact email or phone first). Phone numbers match on their last 10 digits, so formatting does not matter
  (`people.phone_key`, added by `npm run migrate`). Front-desk roles also see whether a waiver is on file, what is valid today, and whether gear is out.
- A sale or booking that arrives with a name and phone number now reuses the existing customer instead of creating a duplicate
  (matching: email, then name + date of birth, then name + phone).

### Gate: RFID cards and gate hardware

- **Two gates, one pass, same moment:** the ticket or card is locked before anything is read, so only one lets the guest through and the other answers `duplicate`.
- **One rule for every scan.** A barcode, a card tap at the counter or a gate asking online all go through the same check (`src/lib/access.js`): valid dates, rides left, voided, pass-back hold, and now a blocked card. Every scan is logged with the card and the device it came from.
- **RFID cards** (Gate, Cards; counter, staff and managers): a card is known by its chip serial (UID) and carries one ticket or pass at a time; it can be loaded again next season. Load a card by scanning the ticket's code and tapping the card. A card that already carries another good ticket is not overwritten unless Replace is ticked. Moving a ticket to a new card frees the old one. **Lost** blocks a card at once (`card_blocked` at every gate); only a manager unblocks it.
- **Reading cards with what you have.** A desk RFID reader that types like a keyboard works in the Gate scan box and every card field. The server accepts the serial as hex (with or without colons) or as a decimal number, each in either byte order, so readers set up differently still match the same card; a ten-digit number is read as decimal first. When one reading of a chip is blocked, the card is blocked. On Chrome for Android, **Tap cards on this phone** reads cards through Web NFC; where the browser has BarcodeDetector, **Scan with the camera** reads barcodes and QR codes.
- **Gates, turnstiles and handheld readers** (Gate, Devices; managers): added with a name and location, then paired with a one-time code (30 minutes) traded for the device's own key, like the time clock tablet. A switched-off or removed device is shut out at once. Settings: how long a gate stays open, and the pass-back hold.
- **Device API** (`/api/v1/device`, `Authorization: Device <key>`): `POST /pair`, `GET /ping`, `POST /check` (one tap decided online; `client_ref` makes a retry return the first answer and never use a second ride), `GET /list` (for deciding offline: today's good chips, ticket codes as keyed hashes only, blocked chips, no names; `If-None-Match` gets 304 while nothing changed) and `POST /scans` (scans decided offline, saved once each at the time they happened, reusing the `client_ref` of a check that timed out; an allowed scan uses a ride; times more than 5 minutes ahead or 14 days back are rejected). Rate limited; closed when the Gate app is off. The guide for installers is `docs/ACCESS-API.md`.
- **Not yet:** direct links to Axess or SKIDATA gate systems. Those need a partnership and their interface documents; gates from other makers, or a reader on a small computer driving a relay, can use the device API today.
- Fixed on the way: a punch card's "rides left" on the Gate screen showed the number before the ride was taken.
- Run `npm run migrate` when upgrading: it adds `rfid_cards`, `access_devices`, the card, device and offline columns on `scans`, and the gate open time.

### Online shop

A public page at `/shop/<mountain>` where guests buy day tickets, multi-day tickets and season passes by card.

- **Turn on:** Settings > Online shop, then tick "Sell on the online shop" on each product. Needs the Stripe keys (see "Online card payment"); without them the page says online sales are closed. Demo mountains never take payment.
- **Flow:** the guest picks a day on a calendar (past days and days where every product is blacked out are greyed; up to a year ahead), then sees what is on sale that day with the price for each age group (`GET /public/<mountain>/shop/day?date=`), adds tickets for named people, and can add other days to the same order.
- **Selling rules:** each product can have `sale_days` (weekdays it is sold, bit per day, Sunday = 1; blank = every day) and `online_daily_limit` (most tickets valid on one day); the mountain has an `online_daily_limit` across all products. Days are enforced in the shared price check, so the ticket window and the shop both refuse a weekday ticket on a Saturday. Limits count every ticket valid that day (window sales too; a multi-day ticket counts on each of its days; season passes are not counted) plus baskets waiting on the card page (held 40 minutes), and stop online sales only. Each basket is placed under a lock on the mountain so the last ticket cannot be sold twice.
- **Pricing:** always on the server. Each ticket is for a named person; their date of birth sets the tier (child, youth, adult, senior) and the day sets calendar prices and blackouts. Tax follows the mountain's rules.
- **Nothing is sold until the payment lands.** The basket is saved as an `awaiting_payment` row in `shop_orders`; Stripe's `checkout.session.completed` webhook (the same `/api/v1/stripe/webhook`, `metadata.kind = shop`) then makes the people, waivers (buyer signs for self and children), a normal `online` order, one ticket per person and the card payment. A repeated webhook changes nothing; a payment that does not match the basket is refunded in full.
- **Tickets:** shown on the guest's page (`/shop/<mountain>?order=<secret>`) with Code 128 and QR codes, and emailed through Postmark when email is set up. Money > Online orders lists orders and can resend tickets.
- **Refunds:** staff refund from Sell > Orders; the card part now goes back through Stripe automatically for online card sales.
- **Limits:** up to 20 tickets per basket, days up to a year ahead, 150 unpaid baskets at once, 12 orders per hour per address (`SHOP_RATE_LIMIT`). Tickets are not self-service refundable.

### Forecast data (ai/data)

The foundation for next season's demand forecast, pricing and staffing. Every morning the daily job writes down how the next days look, one row per mountain, kind and day in `daily_snapshots` (`src/lib/snapshots.js`), kept 3 years (the retention run deletes older rows).

- **presales:** for each of the next 14 days, what is sold so far: lift tickets (by product), lessons by product and age band (from the lesson's ages, for example `4-12`, `18+` or `all`), rentals sold and rental reservations held, online shop orders, and the share of units bought online. Refunded and cancelled items do not count.
- **forecast:** the hourly weather forecast for the next 72 hours (temperature, precipitation, snowfall, wind speed, wind gusts and weather code) on the mountain's clock. When a summit point is set (Money, Forecast data, Weather points) both the base (the map point) and the summit are taken in one request; otherwise the one point, recorded as `map_point`. Each point keeps the elevation the service used.
- **staffing:** today's shifts by department (count, unfilled, published, scheduled hours, patrol shifts included) and yesterday's hours worked by department (entries, headcount, hours). Counts and hours only.
- **prices:** the online, day-first ticket prices offered for each of the next 14 days, by product and guest type, after the early-buy discount, with the list price beside it; days a product is blacked out or not sold say so.
- **No personal data:** snapshots hold counts, prices and weather only. `test/snapshots.test.js` fails if any snapshot holds a person's name, email, phone or id, or a key that would carry one. The table is in the personal-data register as holding no person.
- **Each kind on its own:** a kind that fails is logged (`job: snapshots`) and the others are still taken; the daily job carries on. Mountains are taken 3 at a time inside a time budget (`SNAPSHOT_BUDGET_SECONDS`, 240 by default, inside the daily job's 900 seconds); mountains not reached are counted and logged. After 3 failed weather lookups in one run, the rest of that run does not ask again. The daily job shares one such breaker between its day records, these snapshots and the morning brief (`src/lib/wxrun.js`, 2026.11.15), and each lookup waits at most `WEATHER_TIMEOUT_MS` (10 seconds). The morning brief asks for a mountain's forecast once per run for its operations, patrol and school briefs, and remembers a failed lookup. Stage limits and the worst case: DEPLOY.md, The daily job's time limits. Each run logs `snapshots taken` with how long it took. `SNAPSHOTS=off` skips it.
- **Reading:** `latest(db, tenantId, onDate, kind)` gives the newest snapshot on or before a day (`stale` when it is from an earlier day), `range(...)` a run of days, `coverage(...)` which kinds each day has.
- **Backfill:** `node scripts/snapshot-backfill.js [--tenant id] [--from YYYY-MM-DD --to YYYY-MM-DD]` rebuilds from the start of last season to yesterday, as each day stood when it began on the mountain's clock: presales from when each order and booking was made (a lesson or reservation cancelled later cannot be told from one never made, so those run a little low), weather from the day record (daily values seen, not an hourly forecast, marked `source: day_record`), and staffing from shifts and clocked hours. Prices are not rebuilt (old prices are not kept). Rows are marked backfilled; a day the daily job took is never changed, and running it twice gives the same rows.
- **Screen:** Money, Forecast data (owners and managers): which of the last 14 days have snapshots (taken or rebuilt), the presales for the next 14 days beside the same weekday 52 weeks earlier at the same days ahead, and today's hourly wind and gusts at each point, each with a table view.
- Run `npm run migrate` first: adds `daily_snapshots` and `tenants.wx_summit_lat`, `tenants.wx_summit_lng`.

**The weather source (Open-Meteo).** The same service as Day by day (`src/lib/dayrecord.js`, `fetchHourly` beside `fetchWeather`), with the same switch (`WEATHER=off`), key (`OPEN_METEO_KEY` for the paid customer API; the free API is for non-commercial use) and stand-in (`WEATHER_URL`), and the same handling: a failed or slow lookup (10 seconds) returns nothing and logs a warning, never an error. What it gives, from Open-Meteo's documentation:
- Hourly `temperature_2m`, `precipitation` (the hour's total), `snowfall` (the hour's total), `wind_speed_10m` and `wind_gusts_10m` (the strongest gust in the preceding hour, at 10 meters). `forecast_hours=72` limits the hours; several points can be asked for in one request (the answer is then a list).
- The default "best match" blends models. Over the US that is HRRR (about 3 km, updated hourly, 18 to 48 hours ahead) and GFS (about 13 km at 0.11 degrees, every 6 hours, hourly to 5 days). Open-Meteo says HRRR and NAM end in late November 2026 and are being replaced by RRFS; best match moves over without a change here, but forecasts may shift.
- **Limits in the mountains:** a 3 to 13 km grid smooths ridges and valleys, so wind on an exposed summit or lift line is often stronger, and valley wind weaker, than the forecast. Temperature is adjusted to the point's height with a 90 m elevation model (that is the `elevation_m` kept with each point), but wind and gusts are not. Wind is at 10 meters above ground, not at lift-tower height. Not every model gives gusts (GFS 0.11 degrees does not); best match fills them from one that does. Treat it as the forecast the morning saw, to compare with what happened, not as a lift-hold signal.

### Morning brief (ai/brief)

A short read at 6:00 AM in the mountain's own time zone, on days the calendar says it is open and the day before opening day. In every plan; no add-on.

- **Who gets which brief.** The operations brief: owners, managers and the GM (a manager login). The patrol brief: patrol logins on the roster at the Admin, Manager, Approver or Patrol level; on by default for the patrol director (roster rank Director or level Admin). The school brief: instructor logins at the Supervisor or Director level; on by default for the ski school Director. Each person turns it on or off and picks email, phone alert or both in Settings, Morning brief (`brief_prefs`). Owners and managers see everyone who can get one on the same page and turn it on or off for them. Other roles get none.
- **What it covers.** The facts are gathered by code (`src/lib/briefdata.js`), as small JSON per kind of brief, each holding only what that role can see on screen and only for the apps the mountain has on:
  - weather for the day from today's forecast snapshot (`daily_snapshots`, hourly, base and summit). The daily job takes snapshots at 7:30 AM Eastern, after an Eastern mountain's 6:00 AM, so when today's is not there yet the brief takes it (the daily job later replaces it). A snapshot from an earlier day is never used. Without one (weather off, no map point, a failed lookup) the brief asks Open-Meteo for the day at the map point. Plus the crew's latest snow report;
  - gusts against each lift's wind hold limit (`lifts.wind_hold_mph`, set with the lift's type in Lift inspections; "not set" when missing): the highest hourly forecast gust at the base or summit point during the day's operating hours (the calendar's open and close; the whole day when the hours or hourly data are missing). Every comparison carries one line, under the wind section and in the facts given to the model: the gusts are the forecast at the base or summit point, 10 m above the ground, which smooths ridges, so wind at a lift can be stronger; an early warning only, not a reading at the lift. The brief never tells anyone to open a lift or that one is safe to run: a model brief that does is replaced by the template (reason `go_ahead`). That includes "every lift should be able to run", "good to go", "fine", "safe", "okay" and "no wind holds expected" in a sentence with a lift word, a lift's name or wind (2026.11.15). Operations and patrol;
  - tickets sold ahead for the day (today's presales snapshot when there is one, otherwise a live count), and the expected visits: a simple estimate, the average visits of the same weekday on the last 4 open days of the past 4 weeks (`day_records`). Operations only;
  - yesterday's revenue and orders. Owners and managers only;
  - lessons booked, students, instructors scheduled and assigned, lessons with no instructor, and classes over their age band ratio. Operations and school;
  - lifts with open work orders, a failed daily or weekly check, or a weekly check due. Operations and patrol;
  - terrain park features closed. Operations and patrol;
  - shifts with nobody on them and shifts not confirmed (still draft), by department: all departments for operations, Patrol for patrol, Ski school for school;
  - yesterday's patrol cases as counts by area (the run's or lift's name, else "Other places"), from one aggregate query. The total counts every case; the 11 busiest areas are named when there are more than 12, and the rest go under "Other places". Never a name, age, injury, story, case number or time. Operations and patrol;
  - anything unusual: refunds well above a usual day, a lesson or ticket waitlist growing, the online shop at 90% or more of a daily limit.
- **Writing.** The small model writes three summary lines, short sections and up to three suggested actions, each linking a screen the role can open (`src/lib/briefwrite.js`, through `callModel`, so the cost is logged against the mountain's monthly cap). Then the facts guard: every number in the text must appear in the facts, and every capitalized name must be a lift, trail, area, park feature or screen in the facts (the shared name check, `src/lib/namecheck.js`: whole words, so "Grizzly Bear Bowl" is not passed by a lift called "Bear"; a made-up person or place sends the brief to the template, reason `names`). No links. On a mismatch, a reply that is not the shape asked for, no AI key, the cap reached or a model error, the brief is the template made by code from the same facts. The brief says "Drafted by the assistant" only when the AI wrote it, and "Filled in from today's records" when it is the template (the same rule as the drafts on Today).
- **Delivery.** One brief per mountain, day and kind (`morning_briefs`), shared by everyone of that kind. Email through the mailer (one page, with a plain text part, tag `morning-brief`) and a phone alert through `push.js` (the summary line; it opens the brief). The Morning brief page (from Today) shows today's brief and the 7 before it. Anyone who gets a brief can turn on phone alerts from Settings, Morning brief (`/brief/alerts/*`), not only patrol logins.
- **When.** `POST /api/v1/internal/jobs/brief` every 15 minutes (`scripts/brief-job.sh`), and a stage of the daily job that finishes what is left. Between 6:00 AM and noon local time. Each send is claimed in `brief_sends` (one row per person and day) before anything goes out, so nobody is sent the same day's brief twice, even when runs overlap. A run stops starting sends after its time budget (100 seconds; 240 inside the daily job) and the next run carries on.
- **Kept.** Briefs and the record of who was sent them are about staff and kept 30 days (the daily job's retention stage). Listed in the personal-data register (`src/lib/personal.js`).
- **Evaluations.** `test/evals/brief.test.js`: 8 mountain days (powder Saturday, wind day, rain, slow Tuesday, opening day, understaffed school, a lift with a failed check, a busy patrol day) with a fake model. `node scripts/ai-evals-brief.js` runs them against the real model when `ASSISTANT_API_KEY` is set.

### Patient record access: the fresh check (feat/auth-hardening)

Protect browsing of patient records hard, keep writing at the scene fast (`src/lib/patientaccess.js`).

- **No fresh check for:** a draft the person created (the audit log's create row), at any age; and for a case started in the last 12 hours (`incidents.created_at`), when the person created or submitted it, is a responder on it, was sent to its call (dispatch), or shares an active crew with someone who responded to it or was sent to it while both were on patrol duty when it started (each with a duty row that began before the case and had not ended). Being on duty alone is not an exception (2026-11-05); the weekly summary still labels older rows allowed that way as "on duty (earlier rule)". Who wrote the report (`reported_by_staff`) never counts, and only an approver, the creator or someone already on the case can change it (logged with old and new).
- **Links that count:** a responder or dispatch link made within 30 minutes of the case starting (a person taken off a call that has a case keeps the dispatch link they had: `dispatch_responders_removed`, 2026.11.12 review), from before this release, or added by someone tied to the case (its creator or submitter, the owner or a manager, a patrol Admin or Approver, or someone whose own link passes those tests). A late self-add, or two patrollers adding each other later, does not open the case; it is listed in the weekly summary.
- **Everything else needs the fresh check:** passkey, app code, or password for someone with neither, at the records prompt (`POST /auth/step-up` with `scope: "records"`), counted per login. One check covers 30 minutes for that session. Signing in is never the check, and the records check is not the second check for exports or resets (each is asked for on its own). The check is never case permission on its own: someone who may not see cases (a Host) still sees no case summaries or case-tied notes after it.
- **Enforced on the server** for every `/patrol/incidents/:id` address, reads and writes (the case, report check, versions, signatures, photos, scene, witness links, claim file, documentation score, shares, records, lost-person search, kit use, story fill, notes, patients, responders), from the id Express matched; the released pack, the ski area login's approved report, and any case search. "Add me as a responder" on a case under 12 hours old needs no check.
- **Routes keyed by another id** follow the same rule on their case: witness links (`/witness-links/:lid`), records requests (log, release, decide, change, pack, outside link: `/records/:id`), kit use recorded on a case, and dispatch calls (below). Both exports (`incidents.csv`, `patients.csv`) need the records check as well as their own second check, with or without a search (2026-11-05); every export is on the audit log with the file, its rows and the count of cases included, and a search inside one also logs the cases found.
- **The dispatch board** without the check shows active calls and today's; another day, another status or the season needs it. A call that has a case shows its words (description, caller, timeline notes) only to someone who may open that case without the check. A past call with no case is opened or changed only after the check. Changing a case call's words or timeline (details, notes, status), or taking a responder off it, follows that case's rule; sending people (responders, acknowledge, locate) stays open on current calls. Addresses are compared in lower case without a trailing slash, so `/Events` is the same write as `/events`. Taking a responder off any call is on the audit log (`remove_responder`). My calls hide words the same way as the board.
- **Follow-up emails** run the case's rule on approve, skip and send. Without the check the list shows the case number, due date and state, and the patient's first name, email and comment only for cases the person may open; patients' comments on the summary need the check.
- **Supplies and medications:** a case's ticked checklists (`GET /checklists?case=`) follow its rule. Kit checks and medication moves keep the drug, amount and case number for accountability; the free-text note on a row tied to a case shows only to someone who may open that case.
- **Outside links** (`GET /shares`): without the check, the season risk links and a count of case links; who each case was shared with needs the check. Stopping a case link follows that case's rule.
- **Lists without the check** (case list, patrol home, review queue, day log, map pins) show case number, date, place, category, severity, state, disposition and patient count; the summary only for cases the person may open. Chat previews show the category and date only.
- **The screen** asks in place, with one large passkey button first, and opens the case straight after. The prompt names the action that asked for it (opening a record, searching cases by name, downloading cases, past dispatch calls). Cancelling a search leaves the Cases page usable: the search is not run, with Clear search and Search again.
- **Every open is logged** with how it was allowed (`access`) and whether a fresh check was used (`fresh`). The Access log shows changed fields by the names the case report uses (Chief complaint, Allergies), not database names.
- **Weekly access summary** (`src/lib/accesssummary.js`): it lists every audit row that passed the records rule (the rule tags the row itself, so a new kind of open cannot be missed), plus chat link previews and outside claim-file links being opened. Monday morning, from the daily job, to the owner and patrol Admins: records opened, and one line per person and case for every open outside their own work in their shift, with what, how often, how it was allowed and whether a fresh check was used. Case numbers only. Only the owner can turn it off (Patrol, Settings, Patient record access); the owner is emailed if anyone else changes it.

### Patrol roster and schedule
Patrol keeps its own roster and schedule, apart from Staff and schedule.
- **Roster**: everyone on patrol is on the staff list too, marked **Employee** (paid: time clock, hours, payroll) or **Volunteer** (none of those; a volunteer cannot clock in). Someone is put on patrol from their staff page (**Put on ski patrol**, with a rank), from **Add staff member** (tick On ski patrol), or in Patrol with **Add to roster** (someone new, added to the staff list as a volunteer unless Employee is ticked, or someone already on the staff list). Name, contact, rank, call sign and patrol number can be changed in Patrol. **Taking someone off patrol** takes them off the patrol shifts still ahead (left open) and tells them.
- **Opening Patrol**: anyone active on the roster with a login can open Patrol, whatever their login role (a lift operator who also patrols), at their patrol access level; a Patrol-role login not on the roster is a plain Patrol. Taking someone off the roster closes Patrol to them again.
- **Permissions**: each patroller has an access level (Admin, Manager, Approver, Patrol, Host), and what each level can do is set on Access levels. A patrol Admin (or an owner or manager) can also give one person **exceptions**: a permission their level lacks, or one taken away, for that person alone (Patrol, Team, Permissions). Admins always have everything. The roster shows how many exceptions someone has.
- **Schedule** (Patrol, Team, Schedule): the week as a grid of the roster by day, with open shifts in their own row, each person's patrol hours, and one day at a time on a phone. Those with the **Patrol schedule** permission (patrol Admins and Managers by default; owners and managers always) add a shift (times, station, role, note, who), change or cancel it, copy last week, and publish. Patrollers see the published schedule with their own shifts marked, and in My shifts.
- **Who can take a shift**: anyone active on the roster who is not already on patrol, working a shift in Staff and schedule, or teaching a lesson at that time. Free people come first (fewest hours that week), the rest are listed with the reason. There is no availability check.
- **Messages**: publishing sends each patroller with a login one Inbox message with their shifts; later changes and cancellations message the people they affect.
- **Shift cover** (Patrol, Team, Cover): a patroller who can't make one of their published patrol shifts asks for cover, with an optional reason. The request is announced in the "Patrol shift cover" Inbox conversation (every active roster member with a login), and listed for everyone with whether they can take it and why not (already on patrol, working, teaching, not active). Taking it moves the shift to them at once and messages both. With **A schedule builder approves each swap** on, taking sends an offer: those with the Patrol schedule permission get a message and approve (the shift moves) or decline with a reason (it goes back on the list). The patroller who asked, or a schedule builder, can withdraw it. Changing the shift on the schedule settles the request (someone else put on it counts as covered; opened or cancelled closes it), as does taking the patroller off the roster. Requests not covered by the start time show as Not covered in the last two weeks' history; the schedule marks shifts that need cover.
- **Today's posts** lists who is on the patrol schedule that day (and, for days before the split, patrol shifts made in Staff and schedule) and who is not posted yet.
- **Days patrolled** (Team tab): per person this season (August to July), days on a published patrol shift that has ended or posted on Today's posts, and hours from patrol shifts; downloadable as CSV for volunteer credit and the season report.
- **Moving over**: the migration moved upcoming patrol shifts made in Staff and schedule (Patrol-department positions) into the patrol schedule, with the same person, times, note and status, and cancelled the originals with a note. Past ones stay as history.

Every patrol roster member has one of five access levels: **Admin, Manager, Approver, Patrol, Host**. The level decides which patrol features the person can see and use; rank (candidate to director) is only for training and seniority.
- A patrol Admin opens **Patrol → Access levels** to switch features on or off for each level, and to set each person's level. The Admin column is locked on. "Reset to standard" puts every level back to the built-in defaults.
- Defaults: Manager runs the team (roster, posts, equipment, training, reports); Approver approves reports and follow-up emails; Patrol works cases and dispatch; Host sees the map, posts and trail checks but no case or patient details. Admin does everything.
- Mountain owners and system managers are always Admins. A `patrol` login that is not on the roster is a plain Patrol.
- Existing roster members were moved over from rank once: director to Admin, leader to Manager, everyone else to Patrol. After that, rank changes never touch a level someone chose.
- The server checks the level on every patrol request; the screens only hide what the person cannot use. Changes to levels and rules are in the audit log.

### Patrol: the case page in three views, and care from the rescuer's side

- **Wristband and views.** The case page has a sticky bar that always shows the case, its state, the patient, their age and any allergy, and switches between **Care** (on the hill: time stamps, bedside card, checklists), **Report** (the NSAAR write-up with a section list, responders, signatures, sign-off and the Submit / Approve buttons) and **Record** (notes, frozen versions, everything entered in error). A draft opens on Care; a submitted or approved report opens on Report. The view is kept in the address (`#/patrol/case/:id/care|report|record`).
- **Entered in error, never deleted.** Vitals, injuries, treatments, timeline events and whole patients are marked with a reason (`POST .../:rowId/void`, `POST .../patients/:pid/void`) and stay on the record struck through. They are left out of statistics, exports, patient counts, follow-up emails, checklists, the hand-off sheet, the take-home choice and the form answers below.
- **Late entries and versions.** Each entry stores when it was typed (`entered_at`); more than 15 minutes from the time it records shows "Entered 2:32 PM". Each submit and approval stores a frozen copy of the whole report (`incident_versions`), opened read only from Record (`#/patrol/version/:id/:vid`, audited).
- **Assessment.** Primary survey (bleeding, airway, breathing, circulation, disability, with a time), head injury questions, spinal criteria, OPQRST and a stroke check, and a cardiac arrest section, all in the form registry and filled from tap sheets on the bedside card. GCS is recorded as eyes, verbal and motor (the server adds them up), with the baby and young-child scales; pupils left and right; skin as colour, temperature and moisture; temperature in °C or °F (stored in °C); height and weight with units. Vital sign flags use five age bands.
- **Treatments with structure.** Splint and spinal motion restriction record CMS before and after (a loss after splinting is flagged); oxygen records device and L/min; medication records dose and unit, route, indication, who authorised it and, later, the effect seen. The arrest log records CPR start and stop, pads, each shock, no-shock and rhythm checks, and a returning pulse, with a running clock and a two-minute rhythm prompt.
- **Enter once.** The form fills itself from the bedside entries (body map from injuries, first aid at the scene and at the base from treatments, medication text, time and giver, the scene, base and hand-off times from the timeline). A filled field says so and can be changed; submitting copies these answers into blank fields and fills "Form completed by" and the patrol number (new on the roster) from the person submitting. **Write a draft** turns the record into a CHART or SOAP narrative for the patroller to correct and save.
- **Refusal of care.** Every answer starts blank. A patient recorded as not able to decide cannot refuse (the signature and the submit are both refused, with "treat under implied consent and escalate"). Under 18, a parent or guardian signs, with name and relationship, and the submit check confirms the signature is theirs. A witness can sign. "Care and transport offered" is its own field.
- **Hand-off.** The receiving paramedic, nurse or doctor signs on the phone (`receiving_provider` signature with role and unit); the hand-off time is tied to that patient.
- **Offline save.** Bedside entries made with no signal are kept on the phone (`public/js/outbox.js`) with the time taken and an id, shown as waiting to send, and sent in order when the signal returns. The id makes a repeat send save once. Entries belong to the person who typed them and are dropped after three days. Signing out with unsent entries asks first.

The webhook checks Twilio's signature over `PUBLIC_URL` plus the path and answers with TwiML. The reply, in Spanish after AYUDA or a Spanish text, gives the 911 line, a Locate caller link and asks for the nearest sign code (and the patrol phone when set). Texts within 2 hours of the call's last activity join it as guest messages, with a phone alert to the responders (at most once a minute, never the guest's words); dispatch's replies go back by text from the help number; closing or canceling the call sends one closing text. STOP is kept per mountain and phone and nothing more is texted from that help line until START (Twilio blocks the phone until then too, so HELP does not undo it); every text from that phone is still taken as a request for help, and the call says "This phone texted STOP; replies can't be sent until it texts START. Call them instead." An opt-in keyword set in Twilio (AYUDA, PATROL) that arrives as START still makes or joins the call. Dispatch reads the texts and answers in the call's Guest section (Texts with the guest), the same as a help page request. A text that says lost child, lost kid, niño perdido or perdí a mi hijo (the page's fallback says "lost child") is a Lost child call at High with the Lost child alert; a later text that says so turns the call into one. A position is read only as signed "lat,lng" not inside a longer number, and only with the page's accuracy ("±12m"), within 50 km of the mountain's map point, or with no space after the comma when the mountain has no map point ("12.50, 7.25" is not a position). The MessageSid is claimed before a text is handled and given back when handling fails, so Twilio's retry is taken. A text to a number no mountain uses, or to a mountain that is not active, is answered "If this is an emergency, call 911." and makes no call. Limits: repeats from one phone are grouped like guest help requests; 20 texts per call (`HELP_SMS_CALL_CAP`) and 300 an hour per mountain (`HELP_SMS_HOURLY_CAP`). A texted photo keeps only Twilio's address; patrol opens it signed in, fetched from Twilio and not stored. Tables `patrol_help_lines`, `dispatch_sms_threads`, `dispatch_sms_optouts`, `dispatch_sms_log`; set-up in RUNBOOK.md, "Text HELP set-up".

A medication on a case is checked against it, against the patient's allergies (confirmation, and a reason when the drug is named), and for nitroglycerin against a blood pressure of at least 100 within 5 minutes and erectile dysfunction medication. Stock by lot, station and expiry, taken when a dose is recorded (and returned if entered in error); waste and counts of controlled drugs need a witness. Oxygen pressure on checks (below `o2_refill_psi` is flagged); AED pads, battery and a download owed after use on a patient. The daily job flags expired equipment.

### Guest help: asking patrol from a phone (help/guest, Nov 9)

For a guest who does not know where they are, is hurt or scared, cannot type much, or has a weak signal. Routes in `src/routes/dispatch.js` (the request, settings, the call view, messages from dispatch) and `src/routes/guesthelp.js` (the status page); `src/lib/guesthelp.js` and `src/lib/guestmsg.js`; tables in `db/steps/help-guest.js`.
- **One button.** `/help/<mountain>` opens on I need help now (and Ask for help for someone else, I can't find my child). Pressing it asks the phone where it is for at most 8 seconds and sends at once with the sign code from the QR (`?c=R7`, read through help/places' `places.byCode` when it is there) or `?loc=`. No typing is needed. The call's location line is the sign, the place, the mapped place near the phone (`places.describe`), or the phone's position, and fills in from the guest's taps when nothing better is known.
- **Questions, one per screen, each with Skip,** saved to the call as they are given (`PATCH` by the guest token): who needs help, where they are (trail, lift, building, terrain park, trees, parking lot, I don't know), the last lift ridden (the mountain's lifts), how steep (trail symbols drawn in SVG), a lift tower number (a big number pad), what happened (voice typing hinted), bleeding, cannot move or very cold (a yes raises the call to High, never lowers it), and a phone number. Building or parking lot skips steepness and tower.
- **Lost child:** I can't find my child opens a Lost child call (`call_type` `lost_child`, the ski school's type) at High, alerts everyone on duty (where to look, never the child's details) and asks the child's first name, age, jacket color and where last seen. The call's description and location are made from the answers. The school's photo button shows only on the school's own reports.

It shows the status in the guest's words (received, dispatched, on scene, closed), the reference, patrol's messages and a reply box, Call 911 and Call patrol (the mountain's patrol phone). It checks every 10 seconds, every 30 after 10 minutes. It works for 24 hours.
- **Messages:** `lib/guestmsg.js` `toGuest` saves a message, shows it on the page and texts it when the guest gave a phone and texts are set up; `fromGuest` takes a reply from the page (or a text, help/sms) and alerts everyone on the call without the words. Table `dispatch_messages`.
- **Live location:** while the page is open the phone sends its position at most every 30 seconds, or after 10 seconds when it moved more than 25 m, with its accuracy. It stops when the call closes, after 2 hours, or when the guest taps Stop sharing. The call keeps the latest position and when it was taken, and `dispatch_positions` keeps the last 10. Limited to 6 a minute per request (`HELP_POSITION_LIMIT`).
- **Weak signal:** the page loads no app stylesheet or app script (about 40 KB for page, script and strings; about 14 KB compressed). If the request has not gone after 15 seconds, it offers Send by text instead: a text to the mountain's help number reading `HELP <code or place> <lat>,<lng> ±<acc>m <what>` (`sms:<n>&body=` on iPhone and iPad, `?body=` elsewhere) while it keeps trying. A retry carries the same request key and joins the call it made; a better position on the retry moves the call and works the place words out again. The same phone pressing again within 2 minutes (a new key) is a new call linked to the earlier one (`same_phone_of`, "From the same phone as call 1109-03" on the board and the call) and never moves the earlier call. The server never refuses a request for an optional field: text is cut to size, location text under 2 characters is left out, an accuracy over 5 km is capped and marked rough, and anything unreadable is left out with a note on the call. The page treats a refused send (4xx) as a reason to send again at once with nothing optional, then offers the text message and Call 911. If the first load fails it shows Try again, Send by text instead (the help number from the last load, kept on the phone with the mountain's name) and Call 911. The Locate caller link works the place words out again too.
- **Pass hint (consent):** an optional question asks for a lift ticket or pass number, typed or scanned with the phone camera where the browser reads barcodes. The server notes that pass's last lift scan today at this mountain on the call (`last_scan_hint`, `{ lift, minutes_ago }`) and logs the lookup on the timeline. The guest's screen never shows anything about the pass. Three lookups per request in 10 minutes, ten per address an hour.
- **Dispatch:** the call view's From the guest section shows the answers in plain words (yes answers in red), who needs help, the page language, the pass hint, the latest position, and the messages with quick replies (sent in the guest's language). It sits apart from the location panel and map (help/places).
- **Languages:** English and Spanish from `public/js/help-strings.js`, chosen by the phone's language (Accept-Language), with a switch on every screen. Settings: Dispatch, Guest help page turns the page on. The patrol phone (Call patrol, shown with its number) and the number for Send by text instead come from Patrol settings, Text HELP, the same place the sign sheets and the first reply to a text read, written the same way (610-555-0100).
- **Never AI:** answers, messages, positions and the pass hint are dispatch data. `test/ai-patrol-line.test.js` keeps every AI and assistant file away from the dispatch tables and this code. The new tables are in the personal-data register (`dispatch_messages`, `dispatch_positions`, found through the guest's phone on the call, kept with the call).

### Mountain status

- **Grooming**: a plan for each night or day shift. Add trails one at a time, add every trail, or add only trails not yet groomed. Pick a machine, press Start and Finish (hours and fuel are recorded, the machine's hour meter goes up and the trail is marked groomed on the public feed). Managers can clear all groomed marks after the morning report. Snowmaking runs are started and stopped here.

- **Maintenance**: work orders (open, note, start, wait for parts, complete with what was done); lift inspections (standard ten-item pre-opening list; a failed item opens a high priority work order); recurring jobs due by days, machine hours or both, with Mark done and Make work order; the vehicle and equipment fleet with hour meters.
- **Reports**: lift downtime with totals per lift, shift handoff notes that the next crew can mark as read, and the daily mountain report with text to copy or print.

### Assets and fleet (paid add-on)
**Assets and fleet** turns Maintenance into an asset management app for the whole resort: vehicles and groomers, lift components, snow guns and pumps, buildings and their systems (HVAC, boilers, kitchens, pools, electrical) and equipment. It is an add-on: the platform operator turns it on per mountain under Apps. Basic work orders, inspections and recurring jobs stay in Maintenance for everyone; with the add-on on, the Fleet tab there links across.
- **Overview**: assets by group and how many are down, service due, papers running out, low stock, open purchase orders, and for managers this month's spend, the costliest assets and the ones flagged to replace.
- **Assets** (the register): every asset with a tag (A-0001, given in order or typed), type and group, place, the lift it belongs to, what it is part of (a plow on a truck, a gearbox in a lift drive), serial, VIN, plate, year, fuel, registration, insurance, annual inspection and warranty dates, and a Critical flag. Filters by group, status, place and text. Equipment already in Maintenance gets tags when the add-on is turned on.
- **Asset page**: four big buttons for a phone (log hours or miles, fuel, inspect, report a problem), then Needs attention, service plans, work orders with their cost, inspections, meters and fuel, parts used, costs and the details. Meters only go up; a manager can correct one down, and that is logged.
- **Inspections**: standard checklists (vehicle pre-trip, groomer pre-shift, snowmobile or UTV pre-ride, small vehicle, facility walk-through, general pre-use) matched to the type; managers edit them. Every item must be answered and a failure needs a note. A failure opens a work order (urgent on a critical asset) and marks the asset as needing service.
- **Service**: plans come due by days, hours or miles, with a checklist. Create work order makes one open order per plan with the checklist in it; completing it marks the plan done at the meters recorded on the order, once, even if the order is reopened.
- **Parts**: part numbers, bins, places, what each fits, reorder point and amount, and a stock history (received, used, returned, adjusted, counted). Parts used on a work order come out of stock at cost and are charged to the asset; returns are credited at what they cost when issued, and still work on a cancelled order.
- **Purchasing**: managers draft orders (or Reorder low stock for a vendor), mark them ordered, and anyone on the crew receives them, in pieces if need be. Received parts go into stock at the price on the order, which becomes the part's cost.
- **Costs** (managers): labor (entries at the shop rate of their day), parts, outside work and fuel, all time and for 12 months; cost per hour and per mile; book value written down evenly over the useful life to salvage; and a replace flag when an asset is past its life or a year's upkeep passes a set share of its value. Download spreadsheet exports it (text a spreadsheet would run as a formula is kept as text).
- **QR labels**: print a sheet of tags; scanning one opens the asset page after sign-in.
- **Who can do what**: owners, managers and staff use it; patrol, counter and instructor logins do not. Only managers see or change purchase prices, salvage values, useful life, vendor account numbers, outside work costs, purchase orders, settings and the cost report. The older Maintenance routes return only their own columns, so none of this shows there.

### Terrain park
**Terrain park** (All apps, On the hill) tracks the features your park crew builds with, where they put them each day, every inspection, and each area's opening.
- **Features** is the catalog: rails, boxes, jumps, pipes and more (33 types), each with a size (XS, S, M, L, XL), length or height, whether the crew moves it day to day, notes, and a status: Not inspected yet, Open, Needs attention, Closed, Retired.
- **Only an inspection opens a feature.** Status is not on the Edit form and the API refuses it. A new feature starts as Not inspected yet. A pass reads "Inspected, no defects found, open" (never "safe"). A pass on a feature that was closed or needed attention is a reopening: it needs what was fixed and a photo. A manager retires a feature, or brings one back, with a reason.
- **Checklists by feature type.** Jumps: approach, takeoff lip, knuckle, landing, run-out, deck or gap. Rails and boxes: burrs or sharp edges, bolts and hardware, end caps, buried ends and supports, takeoff and landing snow. Pipes and walls: walls, deck, transitions. Every type: signage on the feature and the surface around it. Each item is pass, fail or N/A, and a fail needs a note. Inspection types: morning, mid-day, after grooming, after new snow, after a change, post-incident.
- **Photos** are kept exactly as taken with a SHA-256 fingerprint (like patrol photos), with Verify. Optional on a pass, required to close (or a reason, such as no signal, with the photo to follow) and to reopen. A photo on an inspection is taken that day (the mountain's time zone) in that feature's inspection: an older photo of the feature does not count. Every photo the inspection screen takes is sent with `for=inspection`. The crew adds a photo that follows to a standing inspection from the same day; another day's inspection, or one marked entered in error, takes a photo only from a manager, with a reason kept on the photo.
- **On the phone:** one large All checks OK button per feature on the day's list opens the checklist ticked Pass for a fast pass (each item is still saved), big touch targets for gloves, and Nearest feature from the phone's GPS. With no signal, inspections, pins, rope lines and area openings and closings wait on the phone in the same sealed outbox as patrol and are sent in order with the time they happened; a repeated send is kept once, and a refused entry shows as Not saved with Try again, Fix the time or Discard. A photo taken in the inspection with no signal waits in the same queue (its bytes in the browser's IndexedDB, removed once sent or discarded) and is sent before the inspection, marked `queued=1`; the inspection names it by its `client_ref` (`photo_refs`). Such a photo counts when it was taken that day and reaches the server within three days (2026.11.13).
- **History of every change.** Size, type, dimensions, name, area, status, rope lines, retirement and an area's rating are kept with the value before and after, who, when and why. A size, dimension or rating change needs a reason. History also lists every inspection and the patrol cases linked to the feature (case number and date only; the park crew and patrol see it).
- **Past days are a record.** Crew cannot change them. A manager corrects one with a reason: the original placement is kept (struck through in the history, with who, when and why), nothing is deleted, and a feature added to a past day is flagged a late entry. A manager can mark an inspection entered in error and add the corrected entry; a correction never opens or closes a feature.
- **Areas** are rated S, M, L or XL, or are a beginner or progression zone (features up to S). Placing a feature larger than its area's rating asks for a reason, kept on the placement. An upgrade moves existing levels across: Learning to Beginner, Small to S, Medium to M, Large to L, Pro to XL.
- **Opening an area** takes the supervisor's sign-off each day: entrance sign with the orange oval, Park SMART or code sign, size signs matching today's layout, rope lines and closures, visibility and surface, a test ride on every jump and rail, the supervisor's name and the time. It is refused while any feature placed in the area today is closed, waits for a post-incident inspection, needs attention, has never passed an inspection, or was not inspected today, unless it is roped off (which takes a reason). The sign-off lists each one by name with what to do.
- **Closing an area** for weather, visibility, wind or hard snow, with a reason and time, closes every feature placed in it, each with its own record. Each reopens through an inspection.
- **Late entries from phones with no signal.** A closing that arrives late wins over an opening made before it arrived, even an opening made after the closing's own time; an opening counts only when it was made after every closing that day reached the server, and a tie is closed. The park screen, the day record, the stored area status and the public feed all follow this one rule, and the park screen and day record say what happened, for example "A closing sent at 10:20 AM arrived at 10:41 AM and closed the area."
- **Day record** per area: a printable page with the layout, each feature with its size, status changes with times, inspections with names, the opening sign-off and closures, and corrections. Times are the mountain's, named as people say it (for example, Eastern time).
- **Patrol link.** When a case's location is freestyle terrain, a terrain or rail park or a half pipe, patrol picks the feature from that day's park map. The case keeps a snapshot (name, type, size, dimensions, area and its rating, status, the last inspection before the incident with its time and inspector); the typed field stays as a fallback. **Close for post-incident inspection** (on the case or the feature) closes the feature and tells the owners and managers in the Inbox and by phone alert; it reopens only through a post-incident inspection. Until one finds no defects, any other inspection (a Needs attention result included) is kept in the record and the feature stays closed.
- **Public conditions feed** lists each active park area by name and rating, open only after its sign-off that day. Features and notes stay in the app.
- Who can do what: anyone signed in can look. Owners, managers and staff logins (the park crew) add, place, inspect, rope off, and open and close areas. Retiring and corrections are for owners and managers. Patrol can close a feature for a post-incident inspection. Every change is in the audit log.
- Open the map where your park is once: as a manager, pan and zoom to the park and press "Open the map here by default".

## Staff

### Staff and schedule
**Staff and schedule** (in the owner's and manager's menu; All apps, Back office) has four tabs: Schedule, Hours, Staff and Setup. Managers and owners only; staff see their own shifts in My shifts.
- **Schedule** is the week as a grid: people down the side, days across, a row of unfilled shifts at the top, each person's hours for the week at the end (over 40 marked). Each shift shows its time and position with a department colour on its edge; drafts have a dashed outline. A department filter narrows it. People not on any shift that week are folded away under "Show N more". On a phone it is one day at a time, with a strip of the seven days (each with its shift count; open shifts marked).
- **Add or change a shift** from the + in any day cell, Add shift, or by clicking a shift: day, start and end (a shift that ends earlier than it starts runs past midnight; at most 24 hours), position, a note, and who works it. The person list puts first whoever can work it (active, holds every certification the position requires and current on the day, free of other shifts and lessons, inside their availability), trained-for-the-position before not, fewest hours that week first. People outside their availability are listed apart and can still be chosen; everyone else is listed with the reason (a missing or expired certification, "Already on Lift operator 8 AM to 4:30 PM on Sat, Oct 17", "Teaching a lesson …"). Every check runs again on save. A shift can be cancelled from the same dialog.
- **Copy last week** and **week templates** make draft shifts. People are kept only where they can still work the shift; otherwise it is left unfilled and listed with the reason. A shift already there (same time, position and person) is skipped, so copying twice adds nothing. Templates are saved from a week (by name; the same name replaces) and listed on Setup.
- **Publish** makes the week's drafts visible in My shifts and sends each person on a shift, with a login, one Inbox message listing their shifts. After that, any change to a published shift messages the people it affects: taken off, put on, time or position changed, or cancelled. Messages come from the manager who made the change.
- **Staffing needs** for any day of the week: expected guests are the day tickets sold for that day plus pass holders (the average number of passes scanned on that weekday over the last four weeks), or your own number. Each position with "guests per staff member" shows needed, on a shift (filled shifts only) and unfilled shifts.
- **Hours** is a week (Monday to Sunday) per person: scheduled hours, hours worked, overtime (time over 40 hours in the week, the US federal rule), and Approved, Not approved or Needs a look (a missed clock-out, or a punch over 14 hours). Each person's punches open in place with the shift each was for. **Fix** changes a punch's times with a required reason (the shift end is offered for a missed clock-out); **Add a missed punch** adds one; **Remove** deletes one made in error. Times are typed in mountain time and stored as UTC. Every fix, addition and removal is in the audit log with the old times and the reason, and fixing a punch undoes its approval. **Approve** approves the week's closed punches; anyone still clocked in or missing a clock-out is skipped. **Export for payroll** is a CSV with scheduled, worked, regular and overtime hours per person; **Export every punch** has one row per punch with corrections and notes.
- **Staff** is a searchable list (name, email, phone) filtered by status, position and employee, volunteer or on ski patrol, with what each person works as and their login. Certifications expiring within 45 days are listed at the top.
- **Each person has a page** (`#/workforce/staff/<id>`): status and details (editable), this week's scheduled and worked hours and upcoming shifts, the positions they are trained for, certifications (add, renew, remove; expired and expiring marked), weekly availability (the editor opens with what is saved), dates they marked open on Instructor availability, their login (**Create login** makes one, with a username from their name and a temporary password; or link an existing one), and their patrol rank and instructor profile. Setting someone to anything but active takes them off every shift still ahead (left unfilled) and tells them.
- **Patrol is separate** (see Patrol, Roster and schedule): patrol shifts are built in Patrol, not here. Patrol-department positions cannot be given work shifts; the grid shows the week's patrol shifts in a read-only Patrol row that links to Patrol. Volunteers are on the staff list (marked, with a filter) but not on the time clock or in payroll. A paid patroller: their patrol shifts count toward their hours and overtime, a work shift cannot overlap a patrol shift, and clocking in for a patrol shift links the punch to it.
- **Instructors**: dates an instructor marks open on Instructor availability count as available when scheduling, alongside the weekly pattern.
- **Setup**: positions (department, guests per staff member, required certifications, how many people are trained for each), certification types (add, rename), week templates, and the time clock kiosk.
- Clocking in from My shifts links the punch to the shift it is for, as the kiosk does.
- Fixed in this release: the availability editor and the required-certifications dialog opened empty, so saving them wiped what was saved. Both now load what is saved first.

### Time clock kiosk

A shared tablet by the staff door lets people clock in and out without an account: tap your name and enter a PIN, or scan a QR badge.

- Set up (Settings, Staff and schedule, Time clock kiosk): give each person a PIN, add a tablet, open the pairing link on it, and optionally print badges.
- Pairing codes work once and expire in 30 minutes. A tablet's token is stored only as a hash and the tablet can be turned off or removed at any time.
- New PINs are 6 digits (random ones too); a mountain can allow 4-digit PINs for new staff (Settings, Staff and schedule, Time clock kiosk: "Allow 4-digit PINs for new staff", off by default). PINs already set keep working until changed. Stored hashed, and five wrong tries lock that person for 10 minutes (a manager can unlock). Tablet changes are filed as tablets in the audit log. Badges are derived from the server secret, so they can be reprinted; "Lost badge" makes the old one stop working.
- Each punch must match the person's current state (in or out), and the confirm step expires after 90 seconds, so a stray tap cannot flip someone's status.
- Snapshots are off by default. When on, the tablet shows a notice, one photo is saved per punch, only managers can view it (each view is logged), and the daily job deletes it after the chosen number of days. Nothing is matched to faces.
- Managers can fix a forgotten clock-out; the change is written to the audit log. Entries open more than 14 hours are flagged.
- Needs `x-kiosk-token` on `/api/v1/kiosk/*` (set by the page), `/api/v1/kiosk-admin/*` for managers. Env: `KIOSK_PAIR_LIMIT`, `KIOSK_RATE_LIMIT`.

## Documents

### Documents
**Documents** (All apps, Back office) is a shared library for policies, forms, manuals and plans.
- Managers and owners make **categories** (Safety, HR forms, Manuals) and choose, for each one, which roles can see it (staff, patrol, instructors, counter) and which of those can also add and remove documents. A category with no roles ticked is managers only. Managers and owners always see everything.
- A document is a file (up to 10 MB: PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, images, text, CSV, zip) or a web link. PDFs and images open in the browser; everything else downloads. Replacing a file keeps the same document and raises its version number.
- A role that cannot see a category cannot see its documents, search results or files; they look like they do not exist. Web pages and scripts cannot be uploaded.
- **Versions:** replacing a file keeps the old one as an earlier version, with who added it, when, and an optional "what changed" note. A document keeps up to **10 versions**. The Versions button lists them; any version can be opened or downloaded, and a manager or an allowed role can restore one (it becomes the newest version, so nothing is lost). When a document is at version 10 the next file has to be a new document, and the screen offers to create it.
- **Search inside files:** every file is read when it is added, so the search box finds words inside documents, not just titles. It reads text files, CSV, Markdown, RTF, Word (.docx), Excel (.xlsx), PowerPoint (.pptx) and PDFs. Pictures of text (a scanned PDF or a photo) are read with OCR in English, but only at mountains that have the **Document text recognition** add-on (platform console, Apps list; off by default, and it turns Documents on with it). Without it those files stay "Not searchable yet" and everything else is unchanged; the first 30 pages of a scanned PDF are read. The old Word, Excel and PowerPoint formats (.doc, .xls, .ppt) and zip files cannot be read, so they are found by title only. A manager sees a bar showing how many files are searchable, with **Scan waiting** and **Rescan everything** buttons; use these once after upgrading to read the files already uploaded. OCR needs memory: run the service with at least 1 GiB (`--memory 1Gi`), or set `DOCS_OCR=0` to turn OCR off.
- Files are stored in the database, so they are backed up with it and need nothing extra to deploy. This release adds tables and columns (`db/docs.sql`): run `npm run migrate` when upgrading. Uploads, replacements, deletions and role changes are in the audit log.

### Documents: read and accepted, and storage

Tick "Ask people to read and accept this" on a document. Everyone who can see its category gets a notice at the top of Documents until they click **I have read this**; their name and the time are recorded. Managers see **Accepted by N** and who is still waiting. Replacing the file (a new version) asks everyone again.

## Waivers

Every guest needs a waiver for the season before they rent gear or take a lesson, and Sell, Rentals, Ski school and the Gate show whether they have one that counts.

- **Signed, not just ticked.** A signing keeps the drawn signature, the typed name, the time, the device (address and browser), and the exact wording, stored word for word in `waiver_texts`. One signing can cover the signer and their children; each person covered gets a `waivers` row pointing at it (`waiver_signings`).
- **Four ways to sign:** the public page `/waiver/<mountain>` (at home or by QR code; works on a phone), a counter tablet mode of the same page that clears after each family, **Sign on this screen** on any guest at the counter, or **Email a link**. Staff can still **Record a paper waiver**.
- **Children:** only an adult (18 or over) signs, for themselves and up to 10 children. A child who already has a different guardian on file is never signed for by someone else: online, that signing goes on a new record marked as a possible duplicate for staff to check; at the counter, staff are told.
- **Who is who:** the public page cannot name an existing record. It matches a person only on first name, last name and date of birth together, never on an email alone, and never changes a matched record's contact details. The signed copy shows names as the guest typed them.
- **The guest's copy:** a private link (`/waiver/<mountain>?copy=<token>`) shows exactly what they signed, with a Print or Save as PDF button. It is emailed only when the address is the signer's own, at most 3 times an hour per address.
- **Waivers screen (All apps, Guests and sales):** search everyone signed this season, open one to see and print exactly what was signed, void a mistake with a reason (managers; it stops counting but is kept), and download a CSV (managers). Patrol do not see the register.
- **Wording:** managers edit it under Waivers, Wording, with a sample to start from (not legal advice). Every version is kept. **Ask everyone to sign again** makes all earlier waivers stop counting, including boxes ticked against the old wording in shop baskets and lesson requests still being paid for.
- **Next season:** a counter signing for a lesson next season is signed for that season (current or next only).
- **Privacy:** anonymising a customer keeps their signed waivers (the mountain's legal record) but removes the signer's email, phone, address and browser from them. Merging customers moves their signings.
- Run `npm run migrate` when upgrading: it adds `waiver_texts`, `waiver_signings` and new columns on `waivers` and `tenants`, and archives each mountain's current wording.

## Injury network (patrol and platform console)

- **Opt in per mountain.** In Patrol, Cases, Injury network, the patrol director (patrol Admin), the owner or a manager turns sharing on: region (NSAA regions, Canada, elsewhere), optional state, whether the mountain may be named, their name and title, and agreement to the data-sharing terms (version 2026-10). Every change is kept in a history and the audit log. Stopping takes the mountain out of the next report at once.
- **What is counted.** Patients on submitted or approved reports, not entered in error. Left out, as in NSAA injury studies: staff injured while working, racing and race training, medical calls (illness, cardiac, a prior condition) and no-injury (their numbers are shown, under the same hiding rule).
- **What is shared.** Counts only: activity, ability, age band, sex, helmet (people on snow), cause (fall, collision, lift loading and unloading, equipment and more), where (marked run, terrain park, off trail, closed or out of bounds, lift, premises), run rating, surface, light, weather, month, two-hour time band, outcome, how the patient reached first aid, and injury type by body area. Never names, dates of birth, exact ages, contact details, written notes, exact times, run names or GPS points.
- **Small numbers.** Any count from 1 to 4 is not shown. Once a count in a list (or a row or column of the injury table) is hidden, more are hidden until at least two are and they add up to 10 or more, so none can be worked out from the total. Hidden counts are all marked the same way. Nothing is shown until 3 mountains share.
- **No subtracting one report from another.** Whole seasons only, and a season in progress stops at the end of the last full month. The injury table publishes its grand total only, not row and column totals. No mountain's own counts are published (named mountains are listed by name). A region is shown on its own only with 3 or more sharing mountains and when the mountains outside the shown regions are none or 3 or more. A sharing mountain sees the network as whole-percent shares and a rounded total, not counts, so before-and-after views as mountains join or leave give nothing away. Demo mountains cannot join, and only mountains on the current terms are counted.
- **Rate.** Patients per 1,000 skier visits, where a visit is a ticket's first accepted gate scan on a local day. The overall rate needs 3 mountains with gate scans and none, or 3 or more, without; rates per category need every mountain to have scans. Visits are shown to the nearest 1,000.
- **For each mountain.** Its own season (patients, visits, rate) beside the network without it, category by category, and "Worth a look": groups that are 1.5 times or more of their share of patients than across the network (at least 5 of their own patients). Only that mountain sees it. A mountain that does not share sees its own numbers only.
- **Platform console.** Injury report: season and region; totals, the injury table, every breakdown with percent and rate, regions, and the mountains that agreed to be named. Download as a spreadsheet (each download is logged) or print. "Who shares" lists every mountain's choice, who agreed and when.

## Patrol case review (review group)

- **The review group** is the Patrol Director plus named backups, set under Patrol, Cases, Review by a patrol admin (owner, manager, patrol admin level) or by the group's own members.   - **Send back with comments**: one comment per section, with an optional note. The patroller gets them in a message and at the top of the report, makes the changes and signs off again; the group is told it was corrected.
  - **Escalate**: the owner and managers get a message with the reason; the report still needs a decision.

## Hardening release (fixes from the full app review)

### Money

- **Card refunds can fail and be retried.** A refund to a card is recorded as pending, then sent to Stripe. If Stripe refuses, the order shows the refund as failed with **Try again**; the daily job also retries. Stripe is checked first so a guest is never refunded twice.
- **Webhooks only act on orders still waiting for payment.** A late or repeated Stripe message cannot sell a ticket twice. If the sale can no longer be honoured (sold out, already used), nothing is saved and the card is refunded.
- **Gift cards are never taxed.** Date-change price differences are taxed like the ticket; the change fee is its own line ("Fees" in reports).
- **One-use promo codes hold while a guest is on the card page,** so two guests cannot both use the last one. A guest who goes back to change their basket does not lose their own code.
- **Cash refunds name a drawer,** so drawer counts include them. Old-style `/orders` sales record tax.
- **The daily report counts sales on the sale day and refunds on the refund day,** so a past day's figures never change.

## AI Operations (add-on): prepared actions and the conditions writer (AI release 1)

The add-on keeps its module key `assistant_plus` and its prices. Its label is now **AI Operations (add-on)**, in the app, the console, the billing plan (`billing_plans.name`, so the website's pricing, add-on lines and `/llms.txt` follow) and the site pages that named it. It includes Assistant Plus questions, the guest assistant, the conditions writer, and the weather-day plans that come next. The rule for every AI feature: the assistant prepares, a person approves. It never publishes, sends, charges or changes anything by itself.

### Prepared actions (`src/lib/proposals.js`, table `ai_proposals`)
- A **proposal** is something the assistant drafted for a person to approve: kind, title, summary, a preview for the card, the payload, the roles it is for, status (pending, approved, rejected, expired, failed), source, when it expires, who decided and when, their edits, and the result or error.
- `register(kind, { roles, module, run, preview, editable, check, forViewer })` adds a kind. `create(db, { tenantId, kind, title, summary, preview, payload, forRoles, expiresAt, source })` saves one and returns its id.
- `approve(db, ctx, id, edits)` locks the row, re-checks that the person has the kind's role (the same `requireRole` check every route uses) and that every app it needs is on, applies their edits (only the fields the kind allows, checked for length), claims it, and then runs the handler as that person through the same lib functions the screen uses, so their permission checks and audit rows apply. The first of two people pressing Approve wins; the second is told it is already approved. An expired draft cannot be approved. The decision (`ai_proposal_approve`) and the outcome (`ai_proposal_done` or `ai_proposal_failed`) are audited. A handler that fails leaves the proposal `failed` with the reason.
- `reject` (Not now) has the same checks and is audited (`ai_proposal_reject`). `list` gives the pending drafts this person may act on.
- API: `GET /ai/proposals`, `POST /ai/proposals/:id/approve` (`{ edits }`), `POST /ai/proposals/:id/reject`.
- **Ready for you** on Today: a card per pending draft with its preview, the label "Drafted by the assistant" when the AI wrote it or "Filled in from today's records" when it is the template made without AI (either changes to "Edited by you" once text is changed), editable text where the kind allows it, and Approve and Not now. The Inbox shows a line with how many are waiting, linking to Today.

### The conditions writer (`src/lib/conditionswriter.js`, kind `conditions_update`)
- For owners and managers, with AI Operations and Lifts and trails on. Approving publishes on the public conditions page, so lift staff (the `staff` role) do not draft, see or approve it (2026.11.15); they keep the lifts and trails current that it is made from.
- Each morning at the mountain's draft time (7:00 AM unless a manager changes it), on a day that is on its calendar, the guests job (every 5 minutes) drafts once. The run skips demo (sandbox) mountains and stops starting drafts after 120 seconds (`CONDITIONS_TICK_SECONDS`); the rest are drafted on the next run. **Draft now** on Mountain status drafts at any time (6 an hour per person, `CONDITIONS_DRAFT_LIMIT`). A newer draft takes an older pending one's place in one locked step, so two presses at once leave exactly one pending draft, and a draft expires at midnight at the mountain. **Not now** on the card sets it aside; the toast says "Not now" too.
- It reads lifts and trails open, what was groomed (the grooming plan done since yesterday, and trails marked groomed), new snow and base (the crew's reading today, else last night's day record), the weather logged today, and the day's hours, public note and events. It never reads patrol tables.
- It drafts (1) the public conditions page note, (2) a snow report paragraph, (3) a social post under 280 characters and (4) a passholder email subject and body. Approve publishes (1) through `lib/conditionsnote.js` (the day's public note on the conditions feed). (4) is sent only when **Also email passholders** is ticked, by a manager, with Messages on, email set up and the postal address set, through `lib/campaignsend.js` (the same code as the Messages screen: consent history, unsubscribe link, no double send). (2) and (3) have Copy buttons. Nothing posts to social media.
- **The mountain's voice:** managers paste up to 5 past snow reports or posts under Mountain status, Conditions writer, Settings. They go into the prompt as style examples, each clipped to 600 characters, with their numbers hidden so old facts cannot leak.
- **Facts guard:** after the model answers, every number (digits, and number words next to a unit) and every lift or trail name in the five texts is checked against the day's facts. A draft that fails is written once more with the problems named; if it fails again, the plain template is used and the card says why. Since 2026.11.15 it also checks:
  - each named lift against its status: a lift on hold or closed is never called open or running, and an open lift is never called closed or on hold;
  - the date is not a number to count with ("12 inches" on the 12th is refused), though the day written as a date is fine; a 24-hour hour ("16") is refused too;
  - names as whole words, and every capitalized word must be the mountain, a lift, trail or event, a manager's note, a weekday, month or holiday, or an ordinary word starting a sentence (`src/lib/namecheck.js`, shared with the brief): a made-up person or place is refused;
  - no links or web or email addresses, and the email subject is one line (CR and LF are taken out of the model's subject and of an approver's edit).
- **No AI:** with no assistant key, the month's allowance used, or the model unavailable, the writer fills a plain template from the same facts. Every model call goes through `callModel` and is logged in `assistant_log` (page `conditions`) with its cost against the monthly cap.
- The day's public note on its own: `PUT /schedule/days/:date/note`, owners and managers only (lift staff get 403; it changes a public page). `lib/conditionsnote.js` is the one function that writes it, for the route and for an approved draft.
- API: `POST /ai/conditions/draft`, `GET/PUT /ai/conditions/settings` (`auto_on`, `draft_at`, `voice`). Table `conditions_writer_settings`.
- Evaluations: `test/evals/conditions.test.js` runs six mornings (powder day, wind hold, rain, early season with one lift, closed day, holiday) against a stand-in model. `ASSISTANT_API_KEY=... node scripts/ai-evals.js` runs the same fixtures (`scripts/ai-evals-conditions.js`) against the real model and prints pass and fail.
- Patient records: `test/proposals.test.js` fails if the proposals layer or the writer requires patrol code (at any depth) or names a patrol table.

## Municipal edition

Release 1 adds a municipal edition for small boroughs, townships and water and sewer authorities, on the same platform. This part is the foundation every municipal app builds on.

### Ski area or municipality (`tenants.org_type`)

- Every tenant is a `mountain` (a ski area, the default; every tenant made before this is one) or a `municipality`. The platform operator picks Ski area or Municipality in the console's Add a mountain form (`POST /platform/tenants` with `org_type`; the bootstrap `POST /tenants` takes it too). It never changes: `PATCH /platform/tenants/:id` with `org_type` is refused with "The organization type is set when the tenant is created and cannot be changed". The console's tenant list has a Type column and the Manage dialog names it.
- `GET /auth/me` returns `org_type`. In the app, `isMuni()` and `orgWord()` ("mountain" or "municipality") in `public/js/ui.js` are what shared screens use.
- The website's self-serve trial stays ski only.

### Apps by kind (`src/lib/modules.js`)

- Each app has `orgs`: the kinds it is for. Six municipal apps, never on for a mountain: Resident requests (`requests`: `/requests`, `/street-notices`; public pages `report`, `open-requests`, `alerts`, `streets`; part of the core), and the add-ons Water (`/water`), Sewer (`/sewer`), Stormwater (`/stormwater`), Trash and recycling (`solidwaste`: `/solid-waste`, public `bulk-pickup`) and Buildings (`/buildings`).
- Shared with a municipality: Assets and fleet (on by default and part of the core there, still an add-on for a mountain), Staff and schedule, Documents, Messages, and the add-ons Document text recognition and AI Operations. Every other app is ski only.
- `settle(want, org)`, `apply(current, changes, org)` and `stateOf` (which caches the kind) follow the tenant's kind: an app not for it is always off. With no saved choice a municipality has Assets and fleet, Staff and schedule, Documents, Messages and Resident requests on, and its add-ons off.
- A municipality's API calls to a ski app's paths get 403 `module_disabled` ("... is not turned on for your municipality"); a mountain's calls to a municipal app's paths get the same. Public pages: a ski page word on a municipality (including the waiver and lost and found pages, which have no app of their own) and a municipal word on a mountain are "Page not found".
- Assets and fleet no longer needs Lifts and trails. Work orders and service plans (`/ops/work-orders`, `/ops/pm`) open with Assets and fleet on, for a municipality and for a mountain without Lifts and trails; nothing else under `/ops` does.
- The console lists only the apps for the tenant's kind (`GET /platform/modules?org_type=`), and refuses turning on another kind's app (on creation and under Apps).

### Roles, menus and screens for a municipality

- Four roles: Owner, Manager, Crew (`staff`) and Front desk (`counter`). `POST /users` and `PATCH /users/:id` refuse patrol, instructor and the ski area login for a municipality (400, `role_not_offered`). Settings, Team and logins offers only those roles, with those names, and has no Menus tab or Season end.
- Fixed menus: owner and manager Requests, Work orders, Assets and fleet, Staff and schedule; Crew Work orders, Requests, Assets and fleet; Front desk Requests, Bulk pickup, Street notices. All apps holds Residents (Requests, Street notices, Bulk pickup), Public works (Work orders, Assets and fleet, Programs, Buildings) and Back office (Staff and schedule, Messages, Documents). Home is Today and Inbox, and My shifts with Staff and schedule on. No ski app or ski group shows.
- Views (`public/js/main.js`): `requests`, `notices`, `works`, `assetprograms`, `bulk`, `buildings`, each gated by its app (`VIEW_MODULE`) and role. The feature apps start as stub pages that their own work replaces.
- Settings shows Your profile, Morning brief, Municipality details (name, phone, address, time zone), Team and logins, Billing and plan, Payments (no card readers), Staff and schedule, Messages and Streets. The ski apps' pages never show.

### Today for a municipality

- A greeting with the date, then one card per thing to look at from each app that is on. Every municipal app answers `GET /<its prefix>/today` with `{ cards: [{ title, count, text, href }] }` (`src/lib/today.js`: `card()` and `todayRoute()`). An app whose endpoint is not there, or fails, is left out.
- Work orders: `GET /ops/work-orders/today` gives Open work orders (with how many are urgent), Overdue work orders, Done today, and Service plans due this week. Each card opens Work orders.

### Work orders for a municipality (`#/works`)

- The same work orders and service plans as Mountain status, Maintenance, without lifts or ski words: tabs Open, Done and Service plans. A work order has a priority, the asset (from Assets and fleet), where it is, who it is assigned to (managers pick from Staff and schedule) and a due date. Start work, Waiting for parts, Mark done (what was done, labor hours, parts), Reopen, Cancel job; managers edit and delete.
- Filters: priority, assigned to (anyone, me, nobody, a person), where it came from (staff, service plan, inspection, resident request, program, street notice) and overdue only. `GET /ops/work-orders` takes `priority`, `assigned_staff_id` (or `none`), `source` and `overdue=1`.
- Service plans by days, hours or miles, with a checklist; Make work order and Mark done.

### Streets (`streets` table, `src/routes/streets.js`)

- A municipality's street list, picked from by requests, bulk pickup and street notices. Signed in, with Resident requests on: `GET /requests/streets` (in use; `?all=1` for all), and for managers `POST /requests/streets` (`name`, `aliases`), `PATCH /requests/streets/:id` (rename, other spellings, `active`) and `POST /requests/streets/import` (`text`, `format` `lines` or `csv` with `name,aliases`). A street is never deleted, only marked not in use. Names are unique per municipality in any case; changes are in the audit log.
- Public: `GET /public/<slug>/streets` gives `{ streets: [names in use] }` and nothing else, for a municipality with Resident requests on.
- Settings, Streets: add, import (paste), edit, mark not in use.

### Plans for municipalities

- `billing_plans.org_type`. Public Works (`muni-core`: Assets and fleet, Staff and schedule, Documents, Messages, Resident requests) $400 a month or $4,800 a year, and the add-ons Water and Sewer ($200 / $2,400 each), Stormwater ($100 / $1,200), Trash and recycling ($150 / $1,800) and Buildings ($100 / $1,200). Yearly is twelve times monthly.
- The website's Pricing page and llms.txt, the outreach emails and the ski Billing screen list only ski area plans. A municipality's Billing screen lists only municipal ones, yearly prices first, with no season billing. Choosing the other kind's plan is refused, in the app and in the console; a plan's apps must be for its kind.

### Assets: water, sewer and stormwater (muni release 1)

Built on Assets and fleet, so most of it serves a ski area too. Migration step file `db/steps/muni-assets.js`.

- **Condition, criticality and risk** (every tenant). The register (`ops_assets`) and the map (`map_features`) have `condition_score` (1 new or like new, 2 good, 3 fair, 4 poor, 5 failed or failing; the column is not called `condition` because that is a reserved word in MySQL), `criticality` (1 low, 2 minor, 3 moderate, 4 high, 5 severe) and `ownership` (ours, the authority, private, the state, someone else); the register also has `material`, `size` and `installed_year`. Risk is condition times criticality (1 to 25), worked out every time and never stored: 1 to 5 Low, 6 to 11 Medium, 12 to 19 High, 20 to 25 Very high (`lib/mapfeatures.js` `riskOf`). Edited on the asset page and the map item's panel (the crew can score). `GET /assets/register` takes `risk=medium|high|very_high` (that level or above) and `sort=risk`. `GET /assets/risk` (`limit`, default 25, up to 200) is the highest risk on the register and the map together, shown as Highest risk on the Overview; the map colors by risk and can show only higher risk. A failed inspection opens an urgent work order on a critical asset or anything with criticality 4 or 5.
- **Map kinds for a municipality** (`lib/mapfeatures.js`). Fire hydrant, Service line, Curb stop (group water: the Water add-on); Sewer manhole, Pump station (`lift_station`), Force main (sewer: the Sewer add-on); Storm outfall, Storm inlet, Storm pipe, Stormwater facility (`bmp`, an area or a point; stormwater: the Stormwater add-on); Streetlight and Street sign (streets: core). Each has a geometry and its APWA color (drinking water blue, sewer green, a separate stormwater green, power red). A kind whose add-on is off is refused with the reason, and is not offered; a ski area never gets them. The snowmaking kinds (snowmaking hydrant, water and air lines, compressor, air valve, low-point drain) are a ski area's only. The older utility kinds (valves, meters, vaults, water and sewer lines, cleanouts, catch basins, culverts, ditches, buildings and the rest) are for both; for a municipality a valve, meter, vault, pump, pump house, intake and tank are drawn as drinking water and a pond as stormwater. `GET /assets/map` gives `kinds` (with `group`, `geometries`), only the `systems` in use, `groups`, the condition, criticality, ownership and risk words, and each feature's `tag`, scores, `risk`, `open_orders` and `last_inspected`. `GET /assets/map/view` is the small map setup (tile layers, where it opens, systems, kinds).
- **A map item's tag and panel.** `map_features.tag` (one per tenant, "V-114"). `GET /assets/map/features/:id`: the item, its inspections (newest first, each with the condition score it gave), last inspection, work orders (open first), photos, programs it is in, and a pump station's `run_hours`. The panel (Open in a marker's popup, or `#/assets/map/f<id>`) shows these with a small map, the condition and criticality to change, Inspect, Open a work order and Edit. A map item with inspections, work orders, photos or a program is not deleted ("Set its status to Abandoned in place instead").
- **Work orders on map items** (every tenant). `work_orders.map_feature_id`, `program_id` and `request_id` (the last for Resident requests), and `source` gains `request`, `program` and `notice`. `POST /assets/map/features/:id/work-orders` (`title`, `description`, `priority`, `assigned_staff_id`, `due_date`, `out_of_service` marks the item out of service). `POST /ops/work-orders` and `PATCH` take `map_feature_id`; `GET /ops/work-orders` filters by `map_feature_id` and `program_id`; a work order gives `feature_name`, `feature_tag`, `feature_kind`, its anchor, and on `GET /ops/work-orders/:id` its `feature_points` and kind words, for the work order's mini map. The map rings items with open work orders.
- **Inspections with measured fields** (`lib/checkrules.js`, `lib/assetinspect.js`). Checklist steps can be pass or fail, a reading (`number`: `unit`, `min`, `max`; outside fails by itself; `run_hours` marks a pump's hour meter), a date, a `choice` (`options`, `fail_options` that fail by themselves), `yesno` (`fail_on` yes or no), `text`, or `condition` (1 to 5, `fail_at` optional). Any step can need a photo (`photo: true`): its answer carries the `photo_id` of a photo taken of the same asset or item and not part of another inspection. Old checklists work unchanged. A checklist has `map_kinds` and `sets_condition` (needs a condition step): the score given becomes the asset's or item's condition, and each inspection keeps its own (`asset_inspections.condition_score`). `asset_inspections.asset_id` may be empty when `map_feature_id` is set (one of the two, checked by the app). `POST /assets/map/features/:id/inspections`; any failure opens a work order on the item (source `inspection`). `GET /assets/templates` lists checklists.
- **Built-in checklist packs** (`lib/templatepacks.js`). Water: Valve exercise; Hydrant flush and inspection (static and residual pressure at least 20 psi, chlorine residual 0.2 to 4 mg/L). Sewer: Manhole inspection; Pump station round (two pump hour meters, wet well level, alarms, generator fuel at least 50%, odor, check valves, security). Stormwater: Outfall dry-weather screening (photo required on flow present; suspected illicit discharge yes fails); Inlet inspection; Stormwater facility inspection. Given to a municipality once when the add-on is on: when the operator turns it on, at creation, when a plan turns it on, and as a safety net whenever the Assets screens load. `asset_template_packs` records each pack given, so an edited, renamed or retired checklist never comes back and nothing doubles. The ranges are starting points a manager changes. A municipality's starting checklists leave out Groomer pre-shift and Snowmobile or UTV.
- **Photos** (`asset_photos`, `routes/assetphotos.js`). `POST /assets/register/:id/photos` and `POST /assets/map/features/:id/photos` take the file as the body (query `caption`, `client_ref`, `file_name`, `lat`, `lng`, `taken_at`), kept as taken with its SHA-256 (EXIF time and place when the file has them). `GET /assets/photos/:id` (the original, `X-Content-SHA256`) and `/verify`. Staff and managers only; never on a public page.
- **Run hours by month** (`station_run_hours`). Each run hour meter reading on an inspection is kept. The table counts the hours between one reading and the next in the month of the later reading; a meter that reads lower (replaced) starts again from its new reading. `GET /assets/map/features/:id/run-hours` and `GET /assets/register/:id/run-hours` (`format=csv` downloads it). Shown on a pump station's panel and an asset's page. For the Chapter 94 report later.
- **Programs** (`asset_programs`, `asset_program_targets`, `routes/assetprograms.js`, view `#/assetprograms`). One job over many assets or map items. A manager starts one (`POST /assets/programs`: `name`, `template_id`, `due_on`, `assigned_staff_id`, `crew`, `notes`, `targets`: `assets`, `features`, and/or `pick: { kinds, box: { south, west, north, east } }`, in service or out of service only, up to 3,000), changes it, adds targets, removes one not done, closes it (`skip_rest_reason` skips what is left; otherwise refused while any are to do), and copies it for next year (same checklist, crew and targets, all to do, without retired or abandoned ones). The crew opens it on a phone: a map colored by state (blue to do, green done, red failed, gray skipped), the list nearest first when the phone shares its location, Inspect (`POST .../targets/:tid/inspect`, the program's checklist) and Skip with a reason; a manager can put a skipped one back. A failure opens a work order with source `program` and the program's id. Progress counts done, skipped, to do and failed; Failures lists their work orders. `GET /assets/programs/:id/export.csv` (no resident data). Today: `GET /assets/programs/today`, one card per open program due within 7 days (or overdue) with its progress.
- **Water, Sewer and Stormwater Today cards** (`routes/utilities.js`, `GET /water/today`, `/sewer/today`, `/stormwater/today`): open work orders on that system's map items (and how many urgent), and its items at high risk or worse; nothing when there is nothing.
- **Demo**: `db/demo-municipal/20-assets.js` adds water mains, 60 tagged valves, 35 fire hydrants, 40 sewer manholes, sewer mains, 2 pump stations with a year of rounds (run hours by month), force mains, 18 outfalls, 30 inlets, storm pipes, 2 basins and a rain garden, and streetlights, on a made-up grid around 40.0, -75.5; scores spread for the risk list; Exercise zone 3 valves half done (two stuck valves with work orders, one skipped); Spring hydrant flushing planned; three failed inspections with work orders.

## Engineering notes

### PATCH keeps defaulted fields

zod 4's `.partial()` still applies `.default()`, so any PATCH silently reset untouched fields (e.g. incident severity to `minor`). All update schemas now use `partialOf()` from `src/lib/util.js`; regression test in `test/map.test.js`.

## Known limits

- Injury network: the network report for a season is worked out from the mountains sharing on the day it is run. If a mountain joins or leaves between two downloads of the same season, comparing the two shows roughly that mountain's counts. Freezing each season's member list when it is first released is the next step. Until then, treat downloads as releases and keep them few (each is logged).

- **Rate limits are kept in each server's memory,** so with several Cloud Run instances the real limit multiplies. `LOGIN_RATE_LIMIT` (default 10 a minute per address) raises the sign-in limit for automated tests.
- **Migrations are idempotent steps, not numbered files.** `db/migrate.js` checks what exists and adds what is missing. Adopt numbered migrations before changes that alter or remove existing columns.
- **Patient and incident data may be regulated** depending on how patrol is run. Access is limited by patrol access level and every read is audited, but that is not a compliance claim (see HIPAA.md). Get a legal review.
- **Customer data is personal data,** including children's dates of birth and photos. Get a legal review of the privacy notice, consent wording and retention.
- **The guest help page is open to the internet.** It is off until a manager turns it on, ignores bot-filled forms, and groups floods from one phone or address instead of refusing them (a real request is always taken). Its status page is limited per request: answers, positions, replies and pass lookups. Every screen shows Call 911.
- **Text HELP takes texts from anyone.** Only posts signed by Twilio are taken; every text makes or joins a call (never refused), repeats are grouped, and texts out are capped per call and per hour. It needs a registered number (toll-free verification or A2P 10DLC) before replies can go out.
- **No password-reset email.** A manager sets a new password for a locked-out person (Account, then Logins); the platform console can reset an owner.
- **The incident form follows the NSAAR layout,** a Canadian form, with US equivalents where it helps. Whether it meets your insurer's or state's rules is for them to confirm.
- **One Stripe account for every mountain.** Online sales all land in the platform's Stripe account. Move to Stripe Connect before a second mountain sells online.
- **Daily ticket limits stop online sales only;** the ticket window can still sell past them.
