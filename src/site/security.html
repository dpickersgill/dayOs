Careful with guest and patient information.

You hold names, birthdays, payments and injury details. Verdynt/OS is built to keep that with the people who need it, and to show who touched what.

Each mountain is walled off.

One mountain can never see another's records.

  • Every record belongs to one mountain, and every request is checked against the mountain you are signed in to.
  • Signing in to one mountain gives no way to ask for another's data, by name or by number.

Every release is tested by creating a second mountain, signing in as it, and trying every page and action against the first mountain's records. A release does not ship if any of them get through.

Access by role.

People see what their job needs.

  • Counter, patrol, instructor, staff and manager roles each get their own menu.
  • Within patrol, the director sets what each access level can do: open cases, approve them, manage medications, release records.
  • Each document category lists which roles can see it and which can add to it.
  • Only managers see what assets cost, what they are worth, and vendor account numbers.
  • Turn off an app and its pages stop working for everyone at that mountain, not only the menu.

Signing in.

Strong where you need it, quiet where you don't.

  • Passkeys: sign in with a fingerprint, face or the phone's own PIN. Nothing to phish or guess.
  • Owners, managers and patrol must use a passkey or an authenticator app. You can require it for everyone.
  • Sensitive actions, like a large refund or downloading patient records, ask you to confirm it is you, even when signed in.
  • Shared counter computers are enrolled by a manager, and staff switch in with a personal PIN, so nobody shares a login.
  • A sign-in from a new device is emailed to the person, and seasonal logins stop at the end of the season.
  • Screens sign themselves out after 15 minutes without activity.
  • Passwords are stored hashed, never readable. Changing one signs out every other device.
  • Sign in with a code from your email instead of a password: it works once, for 10 minutes, and a passkey or authenticator step still follows for anyone who has one. A forgotten password is reset the same way, and the person is told by email.
  • Signing out ends the session on our side too, and a session left unused for an hour ends on its own.
  • Ten wrong passwords lock that login for 15 minutes, and passwords already exposed in other sites' breaches are refused.
  • The employee-entrance time clock is paired to your mountain by a manager before it works.
  • Our own operator console needs a named login with a password and an authenticator code.

A record of what happened.

Questions about who did what have answers.

  • Changes are logged with who made them and when.
  • Refunds need a reason, and the reason is kept.
  • Policies and manuals keep up to 10 versions each, and you can restore an older one.
  • Ask people to confirm they have read a policy, and see who has.

Patient records that hold up.

A care record should show what happened, including the corrections.

  • Nothing is deleted. A wrong vital, injury or treatment is marked entered in error, with a reason, and stays on the record struck through.
  • Every submit and every approval keeps a frozen copy of the whole report.
  • An entry typed long after the time it records says when it was typed, and times in the future are refused.
  • Refusal of care and the hand-off to EMS are signed on the phone. A patient who cannot decide cannot refuse, and a guardian signs for under-18s.
  • Medications can only be recorded as given by someone your medical director allows, from your formulary.
  • Records requests are logged, and only the approved version is released, with a release number. A legal hold stops any change to the case.
  • Entries made without signal are kept on that phone, locked with a key tied to the person who typed them, and sent once. Signing out on purpose clears them from the phone.

Phone alerts.

Fast, without carrying the chart.

  • Alerts go only to the phones a patroller turned them on for, and are encrypted from our servers to the phone.
  • An alert says where the call is and what was reported. The patient's record stays behind the sign-in.

Waivers you can stand behind.

Signed, not just ticked.

  • Each signing keeps the drawn signature, the typed name, the time, the device, and the exact wording signed.
  • Every version of your wording is kept, and a mistaken waiver is voided with a reason, never erased.
  • Removing a guest's personal details keeps their signed waivers, the mountain's legal record.

Payments.

Card and bank numbers never touch Verdynt/OS.

  • Guests pay for tickets, lessons and gifts on Stripe's own checkout page, into the mountain's own Stripe account. Verdynt/OS never holds a mountain's money.
  • Only the mountain's owner can connect or disconnect that account, and only after confirming it is them.
  • Your Verdynt/OS subscription is paid the same way, by card, bank account or Stripe invoice.
  • We keep only what Stripe tells us happened: paid, refunded, failed.
  • A double tap is not a double charge: each payment request is sent to Stripe once.

The assistant.

It helps, it does not reach in.

  • It answers from Help and your mountain's setup, and never changes anything.
  • It sees your question, the Help text, your mountain's setup and, with Assistant Plus, only what the asking person's role could already see on screen. It never sees patient care records or patrol reports.
  • With Assistant Plus, it looks up only what the person asking could already see on their own screen. Counter staff can't get sales totals through it.
  • Guests get public information only, and on the family page their own family's places, nothing about anyone else.
  • Questions are answered by Anthropic's Claude through its business API, whose terms say they are not used to train its models.
  • Managers can turn it off, and see every question asked.

Your data is yours.

Leaving should not mean losing your records.

  • Customers, sales, payments, refunds, incident reports, signed waivers and asset costs download as spreadsheets whenever you want them.
  • Turning an app off never deletes its records.
  • If a subscription lapses, everything stays readable and exportable, and patrol keeps working.
  • Verdynt/OS runs on Google Cloud in the United States, with daily database backups.

What we have not done yet.

Verdynt/OS has not had an outside security audit or a formal certification. If your board, your insurer or your state needs one, tell us when you ask for a walkthrough and we will say plainly where we are.
