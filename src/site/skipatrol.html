Patient care reports

Enter it once at the bedside; the report writes itself.

Nothing is deleted. A mistake is marked entered in error and stays on the record, struck through.
  • Vitals with age-based flags, trends and a "check again" timer
  • GCS, primary survey, head, spine and stroke checks
  • Splints with circulation checks before and after, tourniquet and pelvic binder times, oxygen and a cardiac arrest log
  • A full incident report form, with the mandatory fields your director chooses
  • A CHART or SOAP narrative drafted from the bedside entries, for the patroller to correct
  • Refusal of care and the hand-off to EMS signed on the phone, and a one-page hand-off sheet that prints
  • Collision reports, witness statements and take-home sheets

Dispatch and phone alerts

Send the call, and the right phones know where to go.

  • Calls with location, responders and a timeline
  • Phone alerts to responders, with "On my way" to answer
  • Critical calls alert everyone on duty, and can text them too
  • Lost-person searches with areas assigned and alerted
  • A guest help page from a QR code on a trail sign, which always says to call 911 in an emergency

Opening checks and the closing sweep

Every run checked in the morning, every route swept at night.

The mountain can't be called clear while a route is open or someone found has no call.
  • Opening checks: hazards marked, ropes and signs, padding; open, hold or close with a reason
  • Mountain status changes as patrol goes
  • Sweep routes assigned to whoever is on duty, stop by stop
  • An alert when a route runs late, and the time the mountain was called clear
  • A daily log built from the day, with addenda that never overwrite it

Duty, roster and volunteers

Know who's on the hill, and who's kept current.

  • Sign in on duty with station and radio, on a phone or by tapping your name on the patrol-room tablet
  • Paid patrollers can clock in for pay at the same time
  • A patrol schedule published to each patroller, and shift cover requests someone free can take
  • Volunteer days counted against your season minimum, with reminders
  • Access levels your director sets: who opens, approves and releases cases
  • Lift evacuation plans and drills, and who on duty is ready for each lift

Medications, oxygen and AEDs

Only what your medical director allows.

  • A formulary per mountain: each drug's authority, routes, largest dose and who may give it
  • Stock received by lot and expiry, and taken from stock when given
  • Oxygen cylinder pressure and AED pad and battery checks, with low cylinders flagged

Questions patrol directors ask.

Does it work for a volunteer patrol?

Yes. Volunteers sign in on duty without a time clock, their days count toward your season minimum, and they can ask for cover when they can't make a shift. Paid patrollers can clock in for pay when they sign in.

What if there's no cell signal where we treat patients?

Entries are kept on the phone and sent in order when the signal returns, so nothing typed at the bedside is lost.

Can a report be changed after it's submitted?

Every submit and approval keeps a frozen copy. Corrections are marked entered in error, never deleted, and a legal hold stops any change to the case.

Who can see patient details?

Only the patrol access levels your director allows. Counter, instructor and other staff logins never see patrol cases.

Does it replace our radios or 911?

No. SummitOps records and coordinates the call; radios and 911 stay your lifeline. Phone alerts depend on phones and networks.

Do we have to use the rest of SummitOps?

No. Many mountains start with patrol alone, which touches no guest, and add lift checks or the front desk later.

Can we try it before committing?

Yes. Start a demo mountain in about 20 seconds, or ask for a 14-day trial on your own mountain with no card.
