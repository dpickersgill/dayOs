The letter is about a knee injury on Upper Ridge in February 2027. It says the trail was icy and the rental binding was set wrong. Nobody on patrol remembers the day. They don't have to.

  1. Open the case.

    The approved report is exactly as it was approved, frozen, with who wrote it, who reviewed it and when. Every earlier version is kept beside it.

  2. The conditions at that minute.

    When the case was opened, Verdynt/OS saved what the mountain's own records said: the trail's status, when it was groomed, the snow and the weather. Nobody had to remember to write it down.

  3. The binding, the technician and the last service.

    The rental gear was added to the case from the contract: the binding setting, skier type and boot sole length, who set it, when it was last serviced, and the inspection after.

  4. The photos and the witnesses.

    Photos were fingerprinted the moment they were added, with time and place read from the photo itself, and none can be deleted. Witnesses wrote their statements in their own words, and the scene is drawn on a diagram.

  5. Who was told, and when.

    Management, the insurer and a parent when a minor is hurt, each with the time and how. Your patrol director decides which are required before a report can be approved.

  6. One click: the claim file.

    Everything above in one file, released only from the approved report with a release number, and shared with the insurer by a secure link that expires. Every view is logged.

Questions patrol directors ask.

Will this win a lawsuit for us?

No software can promise that, and we don't. What Verdynt/OS gives you is a complete record, written at the time, that can't be changed afterward without it showing: frozen versions, time stamps, fingerprinted photos and an audit trail. What happens next is for your insurer and your lawyer.

Does it work for a volunteer patrol?

Yes. Volunteers sign in on duty without a time clock, their days count toward your season minimum, and they can ask for cover when they can't make a shift. Paid patrollers can clock in for pay when they sign in.

What if there's no cell signal where we treat patients?

A case can be started with no signal, and entries are kept on the phone and sent in order when the signal returns, so nothing typed at the bedside is lost. Photos taken with the phone camera can be added later and keep their own time and place.

Can a report be changed after it's submitted?

Every submit and approval keeps a frozen copy. Corrections are marked entered in error, never deleted, and a legal hold stops any change to the case.

What goes in the claim file?

The approved report, every photo with its fingerprint, the conditions at the time, rental gear and its binding record, the lift's status, witness statements, the scene diagram, who was told and when, and the audit trail. It is released only from the approved report, with a release number.

Who can see patient details?

Only the patrol access levels your director allows. Counter, instructor and other staff logins never see patrol cases.

Does it replace our radios or 911?

No. Verdynt/OS records and coordinates the call; radios and 911 stay your lifeline. Phone alerts depend on phones and networks.

Can our patrol sign up on its own, without the ski area?

Yes. Many patrols have their own budget, so the patrol can be the customer, paying monthly, yearly, or over the season from November to April. The ski area gets a free read-only login that shows the day's patrol in numbers and every approved report, never a case in progress. See the Patrol only plan on Pricing.

Can we try it before committing?

Yes. Start a demo on your own mountain: type its name and it opens on your own lifts and trails. Or ask for a 14-day trial with no card.
