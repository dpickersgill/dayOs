Ski lesson booking software that keeps classes the right size.

The same rules hold at the desk, in Sell and on the booking page.

Age ranges and class sizes by age band

Each lesson takes only the ages it is for, and each age band has a class size: by default 4 for ages 3 to 4, 6 for 5 to 6, 8 for 7 to 12 and teens, and 10 for adults. The youngest student sets the size. At the desk, going past either needs a manager or school supervisor and a reason, which is audited. Online, it is refused.

Adaptive lesson intake

An adaptive booking records the student's needs, equipment, extra staff and notes for the instructor. Only the lesson's instructors, the desk and managers can read it. Adaptive lessons have their own ratio, and extra staff from the intake add to it.

Unpaid roster and Take payment

A student booked on the roster shows Unpaid at check-in and on the board until paid. Take payment opens Sell with that lesson in the basket, and the sale picks up the held booking, never a second one. Revenue reports count only money paid for a seat.

The learn-to-ski ladder

A first-timer package of 3 or 4 lessons with a lift ticket and a rental included. Each check-in issues the ticket for the lesson day and opens the rental to fit, once per lesson, at no charge. A student who attends every lesson graduates with a season pass offer and a code.

Group programs for schools and clubs

Add a group with its leader, weeks, times and children per chaperone. The leader fills the roster from a private link until it closes, and each child gets a waiver link a parent signs. Staff record drop-off and pickup each week, and a different count at pickup is flagged.

Packages and report cards

Packages are spent one lesson at a time on the roster. Instructors tick off skills, move a student up one of nine levels and print the report card.

Children's safety, written into the day.

Each step leaves a record of who did what. To write your own rules, start from the pickup and release policy template and the lost child procedure.

Pickup and release with authorized adults

Each booking lists who may pick up: name, relationship, phone and, only with consent, a photo. Children 12 and under are released to an adult on that list after a photo ID check, and the family is told. Anyone else needs a manager and a reason. A child waiting past the pickup window shows as late.

Lost child opens a patrol call

Lost child asks for jacket and helmet colors and opens a high-priority call in ski patrol dispatch, with the child's first name, age, class, instructor and where they were last seen. Found records who, where and when, and closes the call.

Guardian waivers for minors

A student under 18 needs a waiver signed by a parent or guardian, whose name is kept with it. A child's own name is refused. Families can sign at home, one adult for the whole family. See online waivers.

Live class updates for families

Instructors post up to 12 lines or photos per lesson from My day. Families see them on their lesson page and by email or text. Photos go only to families who agreed, and families can turn updates off.

Ski school management software for instructors and owners.

Building your rates? Read how to build a ski instructor pay scale.

The instructor pay ladder

Set a rate for each certification level, PSIA-AASI 1 to 3 and Trainer/Examiner, CSIA 1 to 4 and CASI 1 to 4, for group, semi-private and private lessons, hourly work and program meetings. Children's Specialist, Freestyle and Adaptive credentials add to the rate when the lesson fits. Only owners and managers change it.

Training pay and My pay

Logged training is paid at each attendee's hourly rate. My pay shows the open pay period and whether it is approved. Instructors flag a wrong line and the director answers before payroll. No line is ever paid twice.

Instructor ratings from 0 to 10

After the lesson, families say how likely they are to recommend their instructor, from 0 to 10. Directors see each instructor's score with the number of answers. Instructors see only their own.

Owner reports

Revenue per instructor hour, instructor cost against lesson revenue, instructor return rate, and first-timer conversion within 30 days, 60 days and the season, each with a CSV. Only owners and managers see them.

Questions ski school directors ask.

What does ski school management software do?

It keeps lesson booking, waivers, check-in, class sizes, pickup, instructor pay and reports in one place, so one booking carries from the parent's phone to the payroll line.

Can parents book and pay for lessons online?

Yes, by card through Stripe, in English or Spanish. With card payment off, the page takes requests the desk confirms.

How do you keep classes for young children small?

Each age band has a class size, and the youngest student sets it. Online booking stops there, and going over at the desk needs a manager's reason.

Who can pick up a child after a lesson?

Only an adult on the child's pickup list, after a photo ID check. Anyone else needs a manager and a reason, and every release is recorded.

What happens if a child is lost during a lesson?

Lost child opens a high-priority patrol dispatch call with the child's description and last known place, and alerts the director and the desk.

Can instructors be paid by PSIA-AASI, CSIA or CASI level?

Yes. The pay ladder sets a rate per certification level, with add-ons for specialist credentials and paid training time.

Can schools and clubs book a weekly program?

Yes. The leader fills the roster from a private link, parents sign waivers from theirs, and staff count drop-off and pickup each week.
