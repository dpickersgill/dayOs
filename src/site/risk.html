A guest's lawyer writes in March 2028. Here's what you send.

The letter is about a knee injury on Upper Ridge in February 2027. It says the trail was icy and the rental binding was set wrong. Nobody on patrol remembers the day. They don't have to.

  1. Open the case.

    The approved report is exactly as it was approved, frozen, with who wrote it, who reviewed it and when. Every earlier version is kept beside it.

  2. The conditions at that minute.

    When the case was opened, Verdynt/OS saved what the mountain's own records said: the trail's status, when it was groomed, the snow and the weather. Nobody had to remember to write it down.

  3. The binding, the technician and the last service.

    The rental gear was added to the case from the contract: the binding setting, skier type and boot sole length, who set it, when it was last serviced, and the inspection after.

  4. The photos and the witnesses.

    Photos were fingerprinted the moment they were added, with time and place read from the photo itself, and none can be deleted. Witnesses wrote their statements in their own words, and the scene is drawn on a diagram.

  5. Who was told, and when.

    Management, the insurer and a parent when a minor is hurt, each with the time and how. Your patrol director decides which are required before a report can be approved.

  6. One click: the claim file.

    Everything above in one file, released only from the approved report with a release number, and shared with the insurer by a secure link that expires. Every view is logged.

Everything that keeps a record standing
  • Every submit and approval keeps a frozen copy of the whole report
  • A review group signs off reports; nobody reviews their own; only the patrol director approves with an exception, and says why
  • Mistakes are marked entered in error and stay on the record, struck through
  • Staff and volunteers hurt on duty: the owner and GM are told at once, with OSHA's deadlines when it is serious
  • Records requests logged and released only from the approved report, with a release number
  • A legal hold that stops any change to the case
  • Incident reports export as a spreadsheet

What a patrol app on its own can't attach.

Most claims turn on more than the patient's care. They ask about the trail, the grooming, the lift and the rental gear. Because Verdynt/OS also runs those parts of the mountain, the case picks them up by itself.

RentalsThe binding setting, skier type and boot sole length from the contract, the technician who set it, the last service, and the inspection after.
GroomingWhich runs were groomed that morning, when, and by which machine.
LiftsThe lift's status at the time, its stops, and the day's inspection.

Questions owners and insurers ask.

Will this win claims or lower our premium?

We don't promise either. Verdynt/OS keeps a complete record, written at the time, that can't be changed afterward without it showing. What your insurer, your broker and your lawyer do with it is up to them.

What does the documentation score measure?

How complete each approved report is against what your patrol director requires for that kind of case, and how fast it was written. It shows what is missing, so the patrol can fix the habit before the next season.

Can our insurer see patient records?

Only what you share, by a secure link that expires, and only from an approved report. Every view is logged, and you can revoke the link.

Do we have to run the whole mountain on Verdynt/OS?

No. Patrol works on its own. The rental, grooming, lift and staff records attach to a case only when those parts of the mountain run on Verdynt/OS too.

Will you sign a business associate agreement (BAA)?

Ask us in your walkthrough, before any real patient records go in. Details are in our Terms and Privacy policy.

Our patrol is a separate organization. Who owns the records?

The patrol or the ski area, whichever is the customer. The ski area gets a free read-only login that shows approved reports and the day's patrol in numbers, never a case in progress.

This page is general information about the software, not legal or insurance advice. Talk to your insurer and your lawyer about your own records and obligations.
