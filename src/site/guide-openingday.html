Ski area opening day checklist.

Every department, the weeks before and the morning of: lifts, patrol, the terrain park, snowmaking and grooming, tickets and the online shop, rentals, ski school, staff and guest communication.

Opening day is the one day every department is new at once. The lifts have sat all summer, half the staff are new, the online shop has never sold a ticket for this season, and guests arrive with a question for each of them. This checklist puts every department on one page, so the general manager can see what is done and what is not.

This checklist is a template. Have your insurer and attorney review it before adopting, and follow your lift manufacturers' manuals and your state's rules first.

How to use this checklist

  • Give each section an owner. One name per department, written on the sheet.
  • Start four to six weeks out. The "weeks before" items take time: hiring, certifications, price changes, printed signs.
  • Hold a stand-up the morning of. Each owner reads their "morning of" items aloud as done, or says what is not.
  • Keep it. File the signed sheet with the day's records. Next season starts from what slipped this season.

The opening day checklist, by department

Lifts

Weeks before

  • Annual inspection and any state or third-party inspection done, with the papers on file
  • Load tests and maintenance required by the manufacturer complete, and open work orders closed or scheduled
  • Operators and attendants trained on each lift, with the training documented
  • Evacuation plan reviewed for every lift, and an evacuation drill run

Morning of

  • Daily pre-opening inspection on every lift, signed (use the chairlift inspection checklist)
  • Wind and icing checked against each lift's limits
  • Mazes, ramps and signs set at every load and unload
  • Radios tested between stations and with patrol

Ski patrol

Weeks before

  • Patrol roster built, with every patroller's certifications current
  • Medical supplies, toboggans and the patrol room stocked and checked
  • Lift evacuation training current for patrollers on duty at each lift
  • Lost child procedure reviewed with patrol, ski school and lift staff (see the lost child procedure)

Morning of

  • Patrol on duty signed in with stations and radios
  • Opening checks on every run to open: hazards marked, ropes, signs and padding
  • Closed terrain roped and signed, with the reason recorded
  • Closing sweep routes assigned for the end of the day

Terrain park

Weeks before

  • Features inspected, repaired and listed, with sizes
  • Park layout planned for the snow you have, with each area's size rating
  • Signs ready: entrance, Park SMART or code sign, and size signs

Morning of

  • Every feature placed today inspected (use the terrain park inspection checklist)
  • A test ride on every jump and rail
  • The supervisor signs off each park area before it opens

Snowmaking and grooming

Weeks before

  • Snowmaking plan for the opening terrain, with guns, pumps and lines serviced
  • Groomers and snowmobiles serviced and their pre-shift checks done
  • Operators trained on each machine

Morning of

  • Overnight grooming finished on every opening run, and recorded
  • Snow depth, new snow and surface recorded for the morning report
  • Snowmaking equipment cleared from open runs, or marked and padded

Ticketing and the online shop

Weeks before

  • Prices set for every product, age group and day, with blackout dates
  • Online shop tested with a real purchase and a refund
  • Daily limits set if you cap opening day sales
  • Waiver wording reviewed and live online and at the counter
  • Card readers, gates and scanners tested

Morning of

  • Tills counted and opened
  • Will-call ready for online orders
  • Gate scanners on at every lift that scans

Rentals

Weeks before

  • Fleet tuned, counted and tagged
  • Binding technicians trained, with your shop's settings chart ready
  • Reservations open, with sizing taken ahead

Morning of

  • Counter stations set up, with sizing and waiver checks ready
  • Reserved gear pulled and labeled

Ski school

Weeks before

  • Lessons scheduled for the opening days, with prices and class sizes
  • Online booking open and tested
  • Instructors hired, certifications recorded, pay rates set (see how to build a pay scale)
  • Pickup and release policy published to families (see the pickup policy template)

Morning of

  • Rosters printed or on phones, with pickup lists
  • Meeting places signed
  • Lineup run, with classes within their size

Staff and scheduling

Weeks before

  • Opening week schedule published, with every shift filled
  • Certifications required for each position current
  • Time clock set up, with every new hire able to clock in
  • Orientation held for new staff, including the emergency and lost child procedures

Morning of

  • Everyone clocked in, and no-shows covered
  • Morning stand-up held with each department's owner

Guest communication

Weeks before

  • Opening date announced to pass holders and past guests who agreed to hear from you
  • Website updated: hours, prices, what will be open, parking
  • Conditions on your website set up to show lifts, trails and grooming

Morning of

  • Morning report posted: lifts, runs, grooming, snow and weather
  • Front-line staff told what is open, so every answer is the same

Common questions

How far ahead should a ski area start preparing for opening day?

Four to six weeks for most departments. Hiring, certifications, lift inspections, prices and online sales all take longer than the morning of allows.

Who signs off that the mountain is ready to open?

Each department's owner signs their section, and the general manager signs the whole sheet. Lifts, patrol and the terrain park also keep their own daily inspection records.

Is this checklist enough for lift and park inspections?

No. It points to them. Use the chairlift and terrain park inspection checklists, your lift manufacturers' manuals and your state's rules for the inspections themselves.

What do guests most often ask on opening day?

What is open, what it costs, where to park and where lessons meet. Post the morning report early and tell every front-line staff member the same answers.

Should we cap ticket sales on opening day?

If limited terrain is open, a daily limit on online sales keeps lines and runs manageable. Decide the number before sales open and tell guests why.

What should we do with the checklist after opening day?

File the signed sheet with the day's records, and note what slipped. Next season's checklist starts from those notes.

This checklist is general information, not engineering or legal advice. Your lift manufacturers' manuals, your state's rules and your insurer decide what each department must do.
