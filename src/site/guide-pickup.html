Ski school child pickup and release policy (template).

Who may collect a child from a lesson, what to check, what to do when nobody comes, and a fill-in-the-blanks policy your ski school can adopt.

At the end of every children's lesson, an instructor hands a child to an adult. Most days that takes ten seconds. The policy is for the days it doesn't: a late parent, a grandparent nobody mentioned, two adults who disagree. A written policy means every instructor makes the same call, and the school can show what it did.

This is a template, not legal advice. Have your insurer and attorney review it before adopting.

Why a ski school needs a written pickup policy

  • Instructors change every weekend. A rule in one supervisor's head does not reach Saturday's new hire.
  • Families need to know it before they arrive. A parent who read the rule at booking lists the grandparent. A parent who hears it at the meeting place argues.
  • The record protects everyone. If a release is ever questioned, the school can show who collected the child, who released them, when, and what was checked.
  • It ties into your lost child procedure. A child who is not where the pickup list says they should be is the first sign of a search. See the lost child procedure.

Who may collect a child from ski school

The authorized adult list, set at booking

The adult who books the lesson names everyone who may collect the child. For each person, take:

  • Full name, as it appears on their photo ID.
  • Relationship to the child: parent, grandparent, aunt, family friend, nanny.
  • A phone number that will be answered on the day.
  • A photo, only if the family agrees to give one.

Ask for the list at booking, online or at the desk, not at drop-off when the family is rushing to the lift. The booking adult is on the list unless they say otherwise.

Checking ID at pickup

The instructor or desk staff member who releases the child:

  1. Asks the adult for their name and the child's name.
  2. Finds the adult on that child's list.
  3. Checks a photo ID against the name on the list, every time, even for a face they saw at drop-off.
  4. Releases the child and records it before turning to the next family.

Changes on the day

Plans change. A parent gets stuck on the hill, and an uncle comes instead. Decide in advance how the school accepts a change:

  • The change comes from an adult already on the list, by a phone call to a number on file, or in person at the desk.
  • The desk adds the new adult to the list, with name, relationship and phone, and notes who asked and how.
  • The new adult still shows photo ID at pickup.
  • A text from an unknown number, or a child saying "my uncle is coming", is not a change.

What to do when no authorized adult arrives

Set your escalation by the clock, so nobody wonders when to act. The times below are examples; set your own, and shorter ones for younger children.

  1. At the lesson's end time: the instructor stays with any child not yet collected, at the meeting place.
  2. At the end of your pickup window (for example, 15 minutes): the instructor tells the desk. The desk calls every adult on the list, in order.
  3. A few minutes later (for example, 30 minutes): the child moves to a set indoor waiting place with a staff member. A note at the meeting place, or a staff member there, says where.
  4. Later still (for example, 45 to 60 minutes): the supervisor takes over, keeps calling, and asks the ticket office and patrol to watch for the family.
  5. At your final step: the supervisor follows your area's written policy for an uncollected child, which may include calling local law enforcement at the time that policy sets.

Never leave one staff member alone with a waiting child out of sight of others.

Releasing a child to someone not on the list

Sometimes the right answer is to release to an adult who is not on the list: the listed parent is on the phone, confirms the friend standing at the desk, and the friend shows ID. Make that a supervisor's decision, never the instructor's alone:

  • A supervisor or manager approves it in person.
  • They write down the reason: who confirmed it, how, and what ID was seen.
  • The record shows the override, the supervisor's name and the time.

If there is any doubt, the child stays with the school, and the supervisor keeps calling the listed adults.

Late pickup

Late pickup is common and costs staff time. Say in the policy where the child will be, whether there is a fee, and what happens after repeated late pickups. Record each late pickup with the time, so a pattern is visible.

Custody notes and court orders

A ski school cannot judge a custody dispute at the meeting place. Keep the policy simple:

  • The booking adult may give the school a note about custody, and a copy of any court order they want the school to know about. Keep it with the booking, and show it to the people who release the child.
  • The school releases only to adults on the list, whatever an adult at the meeting place says.
  • If two adults disagree at pickup, the child stays with staff, the supervisor is called, and the school follows its own policy, including contacting local law enforcement when that policy says to.

The record to keep for every release

For each child, each lesson, keep:

  1. The authorized adult list as it stood at pickup, with any change made that day and who asked for it.
  2. Who collected the child, by name and relationship.
  3. Who released the child, by name.
  4. The time of release.
  5. That photo ID was checked.
  6. Any override: the supervisor and the reason.
  7. Late pickups and calls made, with times.

Keep pickup lists private. The instructor, the desk and managers need them; nobody else does.

Child pickup and release policy

School:   Effective:

1. Who this covers. Every child aged and under in any lesson or program run by ski school.

2. Authorized adults. When booking, the parent or guardian names every adult who may collect the child, with full name, relationship and phone number. We release a child only to an adult on that list.

How Verdynt/OS records pickup and release

In the ski school app, pickup is part of the booking, not a separate clipboard:

  • The pickup list. Each booking lists the adults who may collect, with name, relationship, phone and an optional photo kept only with the family's consent. Families give names when they book online; the desk can add them at booking, on the roster or at the sale, and the next booking starts from the last list.
  • Release. For children 12 and under, the lesson's instructor (on My day) or the desk (on Pickup) chooses the adult from the list after confirming their photo ID. Who collected, who released and the time are recorded, and the family is told by text or email.
  • Release override. Releasing to someone not on the list needs a manager and a reason, and both are kept. A manager can undo a release, also with a reason.
  • Late on the board. Pickup windows by age set when a waiting child shows as late.
  • Lost child. If a child is not where they should be, Lost child opens a patrol dispatch call from the same screen.

Pickup lists are seen only by the lesson's instructor, the desk and managers, and every time one is read it is logged.

Common questions

What age should a ski school pickup policy cover?

Set an age in your policy and apply it every time. Many schools cover every child in a kids program; Verdynt/OS asks for a release for children 12 and under, and for a child of unknown age in a kids lesson.

Should instructors check ID even when they recognize the parent?

Yes. Checking every adult every time is quicker and fairer than deciding who looks familiar, and families accept it when they read the policy at booking.

Can a parent add someone to the pickup list on the day?

Yes, if the change comes from an adult already on the list, by a call from a number on file or in person at the desk. The new adult still shows photo ID at pickup.

What if nobody comes to pick up a child?

Follow your escalation by the clock: the instructor waits with the child, the desk calls every listed adult, the child moves to a set waiting place with staff, and a supervisor takes over. Your own policy says when to contact local law enforcement.

Who can release a child to an adult who is not on the list?

Only a supervisor or manager, after confirming with a listed adult and checking photo ID. The reason, who approved it and the time go in the record.

How long should we keep pickup records?

Ask your insurer and attorney, and write the answer into the policy. Keep the records with the lesson booking so they can be found later.
