One day, start to finish.

Most ski areas run the day on a ticketing system, a waiver site, paper patrol reports and a few spreadsheets. Here is the same day on one system. Everything below is in Verdynt/OS today.

  1. 5:30 AM Before the first chair

    Know what's safe to open.

    Grooming plans, snow and weather, and lift inspections on a phone. A lift that fails its check can't be set to open.

    Lift inspections and maintenance

  2. 7:30 AM Patrol opens the hill

    Every run checked before it opens.

    Patrollers sign in on duty and check each run, and the mountain's status changes as they go.

    Ski patrol software

  3. 8:00 AM At the gate

    Sell it, scan it, move on.

    Tickets, passes and gift cards at the counter and online. The gate scans a code or taps an RFID card.

    Lift tickets and passes

  4. 9:30 AM Lodge and learning area

    Gear out, lessons running.

    Waivers signed at home, every rental item out and back, and lessons booked without the phone tag.

    Rentals, waivers and ski school

  5. 11:00 AM Beyond the slopes

    The zipline opens on its check.

    Each attraction gets a daily opening check, a failed check keeps it closed, and its time slots sell at the counter and online with height, weight and age limits.

    Attractions and activities

  6. 1:15 PM When someone gets hurt

    One record from the first call.

    Dispatch buzzes the right phones, and the care report is written at the bedside, even with no signal.

    Patient care reports and dispatch

  7. 4:45 PM Closing

    Sweep it, count it, go home.

    Routes swept and called clear with the time, drawers counted, and the next shift reads what this one wrote.

    Ski area POS and staff scheduling

Questions mountain managers ask.

Do we have to replace everything on day one?

No. Start with the apps that hurt most, such as the lift checks or the ski school, and add others as you're ready. Each mountain chooses its own set, and an app you don't use stays out of the menu.

We run a zipline and a tubing hill too. Does it handle those?

Yes. Add each attraction with its height, weight and age limits and whether it needs a waiver. Staff run a daily opening check from a phone, and a failed check keeps it closed. Sell time slots at the counter and online, with places left shown and limits confirmed for every rider, and scan tickets at the attraction's own gate. Patrol reports can name the attraction, and statistics count cases there.

Can you bring over our customers and products?

Yes. Set it up yourselves for free, or choose guided setup: we bring in your customers, pass holders, products and staff, set up roles, and train your crew, for one price agreed before you start. Either way your data is yours, and it exports as spreadsheets whenever you want it.

How does the free trial work?

Ask for a trial and we set up your mountain, usually within a business day. Everything is on for 14 days, with no card needed. Choose a plan and a way to pay any time before the trial ends; paying early does not shorten it.

What do we need to run it?

A web browser and an internet connection. Counter computers, phones and tablets all work, and there's nothing to install. If a patrol phone loses signal on the hill, bedside entries are kept on the phone and sent when the signal returns.

We are a volunteer patrol. Does that work?

Yes. Volunteers sign in on duty without a time clock, their days count toward your season minimum, and they can ask for cover when they can't make a shift. A patrol can also sign up on its own, with Patrol only at {{PATROL_FROM}} a month.
